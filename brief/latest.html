02The AI tape: a record IPO, a short report, and the split that lasted a day

Two things happened in the same session. New AI equity found buyers at a high price, and the incumbents lost them. For this book the second matters more, because ~£80k of fund look-through is concentrated in the incumbents.

The debut

The numbers first. Priced at $83, above the $70–78 range. Opened at $112.87 on stockanalysis (CNN and others report $111.50 and an 11:48–11:50 ET open). High $115.00, low $92.72, close $96.86, after hours $97.00. Volume was about 840m shares, roughly the size of the whole offering, so a lot of the float changed hands on day one. Forbes puts the raise at $81.3bn, against the roughly $68.5bn (825m × $83) I used last night. The gap is about 155m shares, so either the greenshoe was exercised or the base deal was larger than reported. Your book is not exposed to the difference, so I have left it unresolved.

The shape matters more than the level. A 34% pop that halves by the close is what heavy allocation flipping looks like when the book is 6–7× covered: demand at the open, supply from allocated holders into it. CBC quoted a strategist calling it a “big near-term win” for a “very nervous” AI sector. The rest of the session didn’t agree. By the close the best you can say is that the market paid up for a new AI asset and sold old ones to pay for it.

Disclosure, again: I am an Anthropic model. I have no view on ANT as an investment that you should weight, and I won’t pitch it. Your exposure comes through Amazon’s stake and through the fund sleeve’s large-cap tech weight, and it is small either way.

The short report

Hunterbrook’s allegation (CNBC, CNN) is specific: IEIT Systems, a Chinese server maker on the Commerce Department’s Entity List, kept receiving updated AMD firmware after it was listed. AMD says the claims misrepresent its business practices and relationships. The stock fell 7.71% to $489.28, the second-worst S&P performer intraday per CNN. I have not read the report, and I won’t pass on its claims beyond that summary.

It matters for the book because the allegation is about enforcement, not demand. This week now has three items of the same kind: Monday’s BIS interim licence rule, the House Foreign Affairs interviews on 1–2 December (Huang, then Nvidia’s general counsel), and now a firmware-leakage allegation against the second US GPU vendor. If Washington’s response is tighter controls on software, firmware or service flows as well as hardware, everyone in the chain pays: Nvidia and AMD directly, and TSMC and ASML through China-linked wafer and tool demand. That is why the suppliers fell with Nvidia today and didn’t on Wednesday. Wednesday’s question was Nvidia’s governance. Today’s was the chain’s China exposure.

03Oil: Gulf headlines don’t move the settle, and diesel has a policy headwind

Brent’s risk premium has stopped responding to tanker headlines. Shell’s third leg is now the weakest of the three, and it is the only one without a number on it.

The tape

Brent Jan’27 settled at $91.98 (+$0.03) and WTI at $83.21 (−$0.05), WTI’s lowest settle since 26 August (Reuters via Yahoo). That is one source. FinanceFeeds had Brent at $92.12 intraday and HDFC Sky had $91.70 in the Asian session, so the settle is consistent with the day’s range. The Brent–WTI spread is about $8.77, as wide as Wednesday’s, and the weakness is still in the Atlantic Basin.

Three sessions of headline decay

On 3 November a US strike on Qeshm added more than $5 intraday. On 17 November the second Qeshm strike added about $2, and the gain had gone by the next settle. Today the IRGC claimed two supertankers with more than 4m bbl between them hit mines and were burning near the southern coast, and the army separately claimed three tankers. UKMTO confirmed a projectile strike with an engine-room fire, but not the mine claims, and the US military maintains the mines were cleared. Brent rose 3 cents. The market now treats Iranian claims as unverified until UKMTO or an owner confirms them, and treats confirmed single-ship incidents as already priced. The things that would still move it: a confirmed multi-ship loss, an insurer withdrawing cover, or a change in the actual crude transit count, which my sources describe as mostly recovered.

Shenzhen: what the trilateral said about oil

Trump, Xi and Putin met for about 90 minutes at APEC in Shenzhen, the first formal three-way US–China–Russia meeting (CNBC, NBC, spotmedia.ro). Their four-point joint statement covers UN reform, a warning to the ICC “and similar bodies,” nuclear risk reduction, and a plan to “increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations.” It does not lift or ease sanctions and does not mention Ukraine. Putin’s aide said the bilateral session touched on the war with no “particularly important statements.” A note on last night’s calendar: I had this down as a Trump–Putin bilateral. It was a trilateral with a bilateral alongside it.

A statement of intent doesn’t produce barrels of diesel, so the real effect will be smaller than the headline. But it is the third step in a sequence this year: Trump’s tariff waiver on Russian diesel and the purchase deal, the IEA’s 15 October release of more than 20m bbl of diesel, and now a joint pledge from the world’s largest refiner (China), the largest diesel exporter before 2022 (Russia), and the US. The policy direction is plainly towards lower distillate cracks. For Shell, that hits the Marketing and Trading margin that has carried the last two quarters.

04Gas and LNG desk view

NBP sold off on the curve even though Prestige reports a large Troll outage, and Dec-26 is now at a discount to TTF. The UK premium was a prompt weather-and-wind premium all along. A correction to last night’s JKM spread removes most of the Asia-pull argument.

Prices

Tradingpedia reports a rise of 2.6% to €82.93. An EnergyRiskIQ page dated today shows €81.15 (−5.24%), which looks stale. The most likely reading is a front-month settle in the €83–85.5 range, up on the day, as the cold forecast for 20 November and the Hormuz mine claims offset Wednesday’s sell-off. Wednesday’s TTF day-ahead was about €80.50 (Prestige). I still have no verified Wednesday front-month settle. The €81.77 Wednesday low remains the lowest print since late October.

NBP (Prestige, Wednesday settles): day-ahead 202.60p, after 206.00p on the 17th, 224.90p on the 16th and 232.40p on the 13th. That is 30p off in three sessions. Dec-26 201.24p (−11.28p), Q1-27 197.67p. Prestige says the fall reflected the market judging the Gulf premium overdone, not any easing of supply. I have nothing for NBP today.

TTF against NBP: this is tonight’s main observation. At GBP/EUR 1.1986 (Wednesday), TTF at around €84.7 front-month is about 207p/therm. Against NBP Dec-26 at 201.24p, NBP is trading at a discount to TTF of roughly 6p. Q1-27 at 197.67p is lower again. On the prompt, TTF day-ahead at €80.50 is about 197.6p, so NBP day-ahead (202.60p) is still about 5p over. So the UK premium now exists only on the prompt, and the curve has flipped to a discount. That fits last night’s revision: with UK LNG sendout at about 76 mcm/d and Norway mostly back, the NBP premium of 13–16 November was weather and wind, not structural. Two caveats. My TTF Dec figure is a Wednesday snapshot, not a settle, and a ±€1 error is ±2.5p in this spread. And Prestige’s Troll figure (below) cuts the other way.

JKM, and a correction. Last night I put the Asia premium over TTF at about $4/MMBtu, using a $31.35 JKM figure I flagged as unverified. Better-sourced prints put JKM at $27.77 on 17 November (Trading Economics) and about $27.88 intraday on the 18th (−3.5%), with physical cargo indications around $26.00. Against TTF at about $27.2/MMBtu (€84.7 at EUR/USD 1.0973), the front-month premium is about $0.5–1.3, and on physical cargoes it may be close to zero or negative. With the spread that narrow, Asia is not pulling flexible US cargoes away from Europe, and last night’s question about December-loading US diversions matters less. Henry Hub is about $3.26.

UK power (Prestige, Wednesday): day-ahead baseload £185.60/MWh (+£5.60), peak £198.20. Dec-26 £168.44 (−£10.54), Cal-27 about £128 (−5%). The prompt rose on low wind and cold, while forwards fell with gas. I don’t have a Winter 26/27 baseload.

Storage

EU aggregate: 70.5% on 15 November, against a five-year average of about 89% and about 82% this time last year (IntelliNews, citing GIE). On 17 November, 14 of the 18 storage countries were drawing and only Belgium was still injecting. I have no print for the 18th or 19th.

Germany: 57.1% on 17 November, down from 61.0% on 1 November. The 7-day average net withdrawal to the 15th was 589 GWh/d. That is roughly 0.24 points a day on German working capacity, consistent with the ~0.25/day mild-weather baseline from Wednesday. It is the lowest level for the date since at least 2011. Policy: the cabinet approved a draft law on 11 November for a 24 TWh strategic reserve, about 10% of German capacity and for emergency use only. The 4% storage-flexibility request goes to the Energy Council in the week of 1 December. The test starts tomorrow. If the cold spell arrives as forecast, the draw rate should rise well above 0.25/day. A sustained 0.4/day would put Germany near 45% by mid-December.

Italy about 91% and UK about 40% aggregate with Rough at 0.0 TWh, all from last week and not re-verified. Rough is still the structural difference. The UK has no seasonal buffer to speak of, which is why the NBP prompt rises on cold and wind while the curve does not.

Supply

Norway. Two reports conflict. A market report dated 18 November says Gassco flagged a new unplanned processing outage at Troll removing about 10 mcm/d, duration not confirmed. Prestige’s 19 November report says corrective maintenance at Troll is removing about 50 mcm/d until 21 November. Fifty mcm/d is very large, roughly 15% of NCS exports and more than the Troll and Visund outages combined last week. If it were true, I would expect NBP to have risen, not to have lost 11p on Dec-26. Either Prestige has a capacity figure (as distinct from an outage figure) wrong, or the market looked through a three-day event to the end-November recovery. I lean towards the first, but this is the single most useful item I can’t verify tonight. Visund (13.2 mcm/d) was scheduled back on 16 November, and I can’t confirm it returned. UKCS output was 109.6 mcm/d (Prestige). I don’t have total NCS nominations for today. The last figure was about 328 mcm/d on the 16th–17th.

UK LNG sendout: no new print. The last was about 76 mcm/d on the 16th.

Qatar. Force majeure runs to at least the end of December. Ras Laffan loadings recovered to about half of pre-war levels in early November, but most of the extra cargoes have not yet appeared in Hormuz transits. One account says Europe is currently receiving no Qatari LNG. Trains 4 and 6 (about 12.8 Mtpa, ~17% of capacity) are out for an estimated three to five years. Orlen exercised a termination right on one long-term Qatari contract on 10 November, citing the continuing force majeure. Its second contract runs to 2034. That makes Orlen the first buyer to walk away. If others follow, Qatar’s European offtake shrinks permanently, even after the undamaged trains are flowing freely again. Today’s mine claims in the eastern Strait are the type of event that keeps LNG transits at about a third of normal while crude has mostly recovered.

Freight: not verified.

Policy

No new Commission or IEA measures today. Open items: the German storage flexibility request (Energy Council, week of 1 December), the 24 TWh reserve bill in the Bundestag, and the UK WCfD consultation (closes 9 December).
