The breakout

Brent crude settled Thursday $103.76, up roughly 2.9% on Wednesday’s $100.87, with an intraday high reported through $108 around 07:00 ET before fading in the US session. That intraday spike is the specific tell: physical traders saw a buyer, probably a refiner or sovereign strategic reserve, lift the offer side simultaneously across prompt and curve, with the curve going deeper backwardation intraday. The spot settled off the peak but the structural message is clear: $100 is now the floor, not the ceiling. The drivers that pushed through this threshold on this specific day:

  • Hormuz has not de‑escalated. The seven‑condition framework from Tehran continues to anchor the diplomatic track; US pushback through the week has firmed, not softened. Qatari backchannel diplomacy continues but has produced no visible breakthrough. The physical Qatari force majeure extension through end‑November for Pakistan/Bangladesh and early December for Edison is now fully priced into the LNG curve and increasingly into the oil curve as the same choke point.
  • Russian refining attacks continue. Products are leading crude. The diesel crack is now the specific lever that validates Shell’s Wednesday $42/bbl refining margin print — this is not a one‑quarter story if the diesel crack holds here through Q4.
  • Chinese fuel export suspension not restarted. Beijing missed the nominal 7 October restart window; eight days in, no authorisations have landed and the physical diesel market in Asia is now short. The G7 100mb release is in week two and physically arriving in Europe; the net balance of these two is still net‑tight on diesel, which the WTI/Brent curve is pricing but the broader equity market only now beginning to recognise.
  • OPEC+ steady from Sunday 4 October. November 31.1 mb/d. No cuts, no adds; the market is reading “$100 is comfortable” as the signal, which supports a sustained three‑handle for Brent through Q4 absent a diplomatic breakthrough.
  • Houthi activity picked up in the Red Sea again overnight into Thursday with reports of attempted strikes on tankers transiting the southern approaches; the specific risk premium on shipping insurance ticked higher in the afternoon.

03Williams at Brookings: “may be appropriate” and “no urgency” — the walking line the market was looking for

The New York Fed president spoke mid‑session and used almost exactly the two phrases the sell side was positioned against. Endorse the one‑more‑hike framework — done, with “may be appropriate”. Walk back the urgency — done, with “no urgency”. Net: half‑step dovish from Wednesday’s minutes.

The specific language. Williams said that “one further upward adjustment of the federal funds target range may be appropriate late this year to support a timelier return of inflation to target”, combined with language emphasising that there was “no urgency” for the next move. This is the walking line: “may be appropriate” endorses the September median‑dot framework (4.1% YE) that the minutes codified Wednesday, and “no urgency” walks back the implied October hike probability. For a core voter and the New York Fed president, the sell‑side read is that Williams is marking December as the specific implementation window for the final 25bp of this cycle — which pulls October out of the live‑meeting conversation entirely and sets up the November FOMC on 4–5 November as a dovish‑skip meeting that sets up the December hike.

Market re‑pricing was modest. Pre‑speech, October probability was running ~17–20% and did not move materially; November post‑Williams probability dropped to around 65–72%, below Wednesday’s 87% post‑minutes but still comfortably above 50%. December probability moved higher to around 85–90%. The 2027 terminal rate pricing stayed around 4.50–4.75% — no change here, consistent with the view that Williams moved the implementation sequence but not the destination. In rates, the near end bull‑steepened mildly against Wednesday’s bear flattening: US 10Y around 5.25–5.28% at the close, down ~5bp from Wednesday’s 5.32%; 2Y around 4.90%, down ~5bp from Wednesday’s 4.95%. 30Y around 5.65%, down ~5bp from Wednesday’s 5.70%. The specific read: layoffs are not accelerating despite the +29k NFP Friday, which supports the stagflation reading rather than the recession reading. For positioning, this is a hard counterweight to the dovish Williams line — the Fed has cover to tighten one more time without a visible labour‑market dislocation, which is exactly the condition Williams described for “may be appropriate”.
