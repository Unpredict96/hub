02Meta’s $35bn equity offering: the credit tape made real

Meta’s FWP filing Monday morning US time is the specific event that turns the Thursday Q3 blow‑up into a cycle‑defining capital structure decision. The company sold the market a story on Wednesday night: AI capex has to go up, FCF has to go negative, trust the long run. Four trading days later it is selling the market the paper to fund that story. $35bn concurrent now, up to $60bn ATM after Q1 2027. The specific details — and the reflexive detail of NVDA, AMD and AVGO signalling interest as buyers — are what the thesis under the book has been running toward since September.

The specific structure of the offering

  • $22.5bn Class A common stock, underwritten, priced through the Monday–Tuesday window. Dilution against the current float at $630.56 is approximately 1.4% of basic shares outstanding on a mechanical basis.
  • $12.5bn convertible preferred, structured as depositary shares. Convertible to common at a premium (strike not confirmed from the sources I could reach), which pushes the equity accounting of this slice forward by 24–36 months while giving Meta the fixed‑coupon obligation now. This is the specific structure a company uses when it wants to look like it is holding dilution flat for the next two prints while actually raising equity.
  • Up to $60bn at‑the‑market share sale programme off a new Class C non‑voting share. The Class C begins trading in Q1 2027, which pushes the programme start into fiscal 2027. If run to its full upper bound against Monday’s mark, the ATM would raise approximately $59–61bn of additional equity between Q1 2027 and Q4 2027. The upper‑bound combined with the concurrent raise is $95bn — which is roughly two‑thirds of a single year’s raised FY26 capex guide ($135–145bn), and the implicit admission that the capex envelope does not close on operating cash alone.
  • Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom have each indicated interest in buying Class A in the offering, for up to $5bn in total. This is the specific detail. The three equipment vendors that will book the Meta capex as revenue are stepping into the Meta equity offering as capital providers. The reflex works both directions — chip‑vendor cash recycled into the hyperscaler whose spending is the chip vendors’ revenue line. For the credit tape, this is a positive signal in the short run (the equipment vendors are confirming the capex demand is real with cash in hand). For the structural question, it is the single specific illustration of the reflexivity the complex is built on: NVDA’s revenue line funds the Meta buyback it will take out as equity.

What the market priced vs what it did not

Meta stock did −1.88% to $630.56 on Monday — a mild negative. That is less drag than the historical equity‑raise discount for a $35bn concurrent (which typically prices at a 4–7% discount to prior close). Three reads of why:

  • The news was partly priced. On Thursday Meta’s Q3 release had already explicitly signalled “a new class of shares” was being prepared for financing — the market had three days to position for the structure.
  • The Anthropic S‑1 pulled sentiment higher the same morning, giving the AI complex a sentiment tailwind that offset the dilution read‑through. The precise coincidence of timing (Meta FWP + Anthropic S‑1 same Monday) is unlikely to be accidental — the capital markets desk sees the full flow calendar.
  • The $5bn equipment‑vendor commitment supplied a floor. If NVDA, AMD and AVGO are stepping in, the mark‑to‑market of Monday’s offering carries an implicit cornerstone backstop.

What the market did not price Monday: the implicit 2027 capex envelope. A $60bn ATM programme starting Q1 2027 only makes sense if the capex is still running at a $135–160bn+ run‑rate in that fiscal year. Which means the raised FY26 guide is not a peak — it is a step. The Wednesday 28 October Q3 night (MSFT, GOOGL, META itself re‑prints Q3 data, GSK) is where the market gets the specific transmission to the other hyperscalers. Microsoft’s capex guide into fiscal 2027 H2 is now the single most important thing in the Wednesday evening Big Tech cluster. If MSFT raises its capex envelope on Wednesday evening, the Meta template becomes the sector template.

The read‑across into the credit tape

The 5Y CDS complex that printed records Thursday did its job today: Meta 5Y CDS was flat to tighter on the equity raise, which is the correct reaction — raising equity reduces credit risk at the cost of equity holders. The specific line to carry is the Wednesday Q3 release: MSFT and GOOGL 5Y CDS reactions to those prints will tell you whether the Meta template is being read as a company‑specific capital structure story (bearish for Meta equity, flat for the others) or an industry‑wide capex envelope story (bearish for the complex). CoreWeave CDS at 1,082–1,088bp did not meaningfully tighten on the news — the neocloud credit layer remains distressed, which is the specific signal that the equipment‑vendor $5bn commitment is going to Meta, not into the neocloud financing stack. Oracle CDS at ~263bp and SpaceX at ~203bp both held flat.

For your bookDirect Meta on 1 share: −£9 Monday. The Wednesday evening Q3 release is where Meta re‑prints its own numbers alongside MSFT and GOOGL, and the specific question for the share price is whether the dilution guide is held at the $35bn concurrent + $60bn ATM run‑rate or whether anything in the Q3 earnings call changes the structure. For the look‑through through Fundsmith (Alphabet weight), ATT‑ex (chip exposure already removed by Tuesday’s exit), Royal London and the trackers, Monday’s dispersion was clean: hyperscalers mildly weaker (MSFT the exception, +1.54% as the Big Tech reflexively advantaged), chip names stronger, Meta direct the drag. 03Hormuz vs the Mecca pact: oil off the peak, physical tempo still at record

The weekend tempo

  • Saturday–Sunday 25 October: LPG tanker struck near Oman’s Sohar anchorage; separately, the Houthis claimed strikes on pumping stations on Saudi Arabia’s East‑West pipeline and on Riyadh airport — neither confirmed by Saudi authorities.

The specific tell: physical insurance is now cutting activity, not just adding to the price. The Mecca‑pact political step

Monday’s Tehran meeting is the specific political event the oil tape read as the ceiling. The delegation: Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman, Pakistani army chief Asim Munir, Turkish intelligence chief İbrahim Kalın. No communique at the time of writing.

The specific framing to carry: this is the first political event where Saudi Arabia has sat down with Iran at ministerial level since the war opened in February. For the oil market this reads as a credible political ceiling — if Saudi is willing to engage, Iran has a diplomatic lane that reduces the probability of extreme escalation (a full strait closure, a Saudi oil infrastructure strike, a US kinetic response). Brent off $117 peak to roughly $114.5 by Monday’s US close, approximately $3/bbl of political premium drained.

What the Tehran meeting does not resolve: the physical attacker is still not identified, the IRGC public force‑majeure signalling remains live from the GFS Pride threat earlier in October, and the Houthi weekend strikes signal that the sub‑state actor layer remains outside any pact framework. The implicit ceiling the market is now pricing has slipped by a dollar or two: $108–115 Brent through the midterms, with the political layer rather than the physical layer setting the cap.
