02The 30‑year gilt touched 6% — the Chancellor’s path just narrowed again

The intraday high is a 1998 print. The Budget sits 27 days from now. Wednesday’s GDP headroom just got eaten by a single session.

What happened. UK 30‑year gilt yields crossed 6.07% in morning trading, the first six‑handle on the long end since 1998. The 10‑year gilt likely moved into the 5.40–5.50% area in sympathy, pending exact confirmation. The FTSE 100 opened down 2.0% to approximately 10,397, before recovering through the London afternoon on a modest commodity bid (Shell, Rio Tinto, Glencore) and a sterling recovery off the morning low. Index close 10,486.22, −1.13%. European equity markets took the same backdrop with the DAX and CAC each down roughly 1.3%.

Why this is not a UK story alone, but is a UK story. The direct trigger was the global bond complex: ISM 54.5 pushed the US 10‑year to 5.34%, Japan’s long end widened on continued Takaichi fiscal signalling, and bund yields followed. In that environment UK gilts always underperform at the long end — the duration risk is higher, and the fiscal narrative that has been building since the August borrowing overshoot provides an amplification channel. The specific thing that happened today is that the gilt rout stopped being a sympathy move and started being read as a standalone fiscal‑capacity concern. That is the point where options traders start pricing a gilt vol event through the 28 October Budget. Wednesday’s GDP revision gave the Chancellor roughly £3–5bn of static headroom on the OBR Q2 base; a 20bp move wider on 10‑year gilts eats that headroom in a single session on the debt‑service line.

What the market is now pricing into the Budget. Three things, in order of weight. First, a £30bn+ tax package that was already in the mark — today didn’t add to that, but it also didn’t remove it. Second, the possibility of a windfall measure on either banks or energy producers to raise cyclical revenue without touching the headline tax rate; this is the specific line that reads directly to UKW and Shell. The gilt move today makes a windfall marginally more likely at the margin, because it increases the pressure on the Treasury to find revenue the market won’t punish on the deficit side. Third, a shift in the gilt issuance programme toward the short end to reduce funding cost pressure, which the DMO flagged as under review in last week’s remit consultation. If that lands at the Budget, the long end rallies by 15–25bp on announcement and UKW gets a significant bid back toward 115p.

What this does to your UKW position specifically. UKW does not trade on gilt yields tick for tick; it trades on the implied discount rate used in the NAV, which references long gilts plus a risk premium. The 15 October Capital Markets Event at The Ned is the catalyst that either stabilises the discount through a buyback escalation / dividend reset / asset recycling announcement, or confirms the structural re‑rating. Today’s move takes the position from an unexciting range trade to a conscious long on fiscal recovery through two specific events: the Capital Markets Event on 15 October and the Budget on 28 October. The downside is roughly another 5p lower if gilts take another 20bp wider; the upside is 10p higher if the Budget delivers a benign package and the DMO shifts issuance. Position sizing (8,349 shares, ~£9,000) is a small enough slice of the book that this is a holdable binary — you don’t need to average down and you don’t need to cut; the position does the work of being short duration directly, with a known catalyst calendar.

The Budget frame — updatedYesterday’s brief said the Chancellor had a fractionally easier arithmetic on the Wednesday GDP revision. One session later, the gilt move has consumed that headroom. The net is roughly unchanged: a £25–30bn tax package remains the mark, with a windfall measure as a marginal possibility. The specific thing to watch between now and 28 October is Treasury committee readouts and any DMO remit statement, plus the 10‑year gilt yield trajectory. A drift back into the 5.20s on the 10‑year is the signal the market has absorbed the fiscal event in advance; a push through 5.60% is the signal the Budget delivery itself becomes a tail risk. The AJ Bell / FTSE close is doing the macro work; your II ISA holds the position directly.

03ISM at 54.5, not 48.7 — manufacturing has turned and the belly got the message

A 5.8‑point beat on top of Wednesday’s Chicago 58.8 and last week’s S&P flash 58.4 is three independent signals in two sessions. The 10‑year ran to a 24‑year high before easing. Friday’s NFP now picks the next direction.

The release. ISM published the September manufacturing print at 15:00 BST: 54.5 against a 48.7 Reuters consensus, and marginally below the 54.6 August print. That is a 5.8‑point beat and the ninth consecutive month of expansion for the index — the longest expansion run since 2022. Twelve manufacturing industries reported growth, including Electrical Equipment, Primary Metals, Computer & Electronic Products, and Chemical Products; only two reported contraction (Printing & Related Support, Textile Mills). The internal sub‑indices worth reading from your Argus / Bloomberg terminal when they clear are new orders (last at 55.1), production, employment, and critically the prices‑paid sub‑index — last month’s S&P flash had input prices at 66.4, the highest since October 2022, and the ISM confirmation of that number is what keeps the Powell inner circle uncomfortable. A print of prices‑paid above 65 with the headline at 54.5 is specifically the combination that gets the December hike locked in.

The curve reaction. Immediate: 10‑year ran to 5.34% intraday, the highest since April 2002; 30‑year touched 5.64%; 2‑year held at 5.07%. Steepening at the long end on the inflation signal, not the growth signal. Both long tenors then round‑tripped on bargain‑hunting into the close as the 2002‑high range brought systematic duration buyers back — 10‑year closed 5.24%, essentially flat on Wednesday, and the 30‑year likely closed in the 5.52–5.55% area. The round‑trip is the specific thing worth noting. A 5.8‑point beat on a Fed‑watched survey, with a 2002‑level long end holding on bargain‑hunting, is the tape telling you the duration unwind that followed Williams’ Buffalo speech is already complete. Fast money is no longer long duration; the next move is picked by Friday’s NFP.

The hike‑odds reaction was modest. October hike odds held around 42%, barely moved from Wednesday’s 44%. December odds held just under 90%. The specific shift is a new 15% probability on a second hike through Q1 2027 that the market was not carrying a week ago — this is the terminal‑rate drift that matters more than the near‑term meeting pricing. A single ISM beat doesn’t move Williams’ stated reaction function; what it does is remove the easy case for a dovish surprise at the November FOMC. The Fed’s own dot plot showed one more 25bp hike as the median call; today reinforces that median against the dove minority.

The jobless claims line that walked past unnoticed. Initial jobless claims for the week ending 26 September came in around 200k, against the prior 197k. The four‑week average held in the mid‑190s. There is no signal of labour‑market deterioration anywhere in the weekly data, which combined with Wednesday’s ADP +90k means the setup into Friday’s NFP continues to skew to the hot side. The ADP commentary pointed to recovery after three months of decline; the ISM employment sub‑index when it clears will tell you whether the manufacturing hiring piece is also turning.

ADP’s +90k and today’s ISM 54.5 both skew the setup slightly hot. A print below +70k with UR at 4.3% puts October back on the table, rallies the belly back to 5.15%, and takes UKW back to 112p. The second number worth watching is ISM services at 15:00 BST (consensus ~53): a services print above 55 with the price sub‑index elevated locks in the stagflation‑pricing regime for the rest of October. Eurozone flash CPI at 10:00 BST (consensus 3.6% headline, 2.6% core) sits as the direct read for Fidelity European through the ECB path.

04Micron did everything right. The memory sleeve closed flat.

EPS beat the Street. Q1 guide hot. HBM calendar 2027 sold out. Partnership with Nvidia on NVHBM. The stock priced in a 7–8% implied move and settled flat. The read‑across for your NVDA / AVGO / AMD exposure is more cautious than the headlines read.

The print. FQ4 EPS $33.42 against the $31.83 Street estimate, revenue well ahead of guide, gross margin expansion sequentially driven by the HBM mix shift, and a Q1 guide that pointed to continued sequential growth into calendar Q4. The three specific pieces of commentary that the memory bulls needed and received: (i) “the vast majority of calendar 2027 HBM bit supply has already been committed at significant year‑over‑year price increases”; (ii) a design‑partner relationship with Nvidia on NVHBM, the industry’s first custom HBM4E implementation; (iii) DRAM supply constrained through calendar 2027 to 2028 with industry bit growth in the low 20s percent. On any one of those three lines the stock would normally rate a solid session.

The reaction. Shares traded in a $1,041–$1,088 intraday range and settled around $1,069, roughly flat to modestly up on the day. Options markets had carried a 7–8% implied earnings move; the realised move was inside 2%. The read: AI‑memory tightness is now consensus priced, best‑case outcomes are already in the mark, and the next catalyst for the memory thesis has to come from a price event (a specific HBM4E contract disclosure at a hyperscaler, or a DRAM contract reset) rather than a guidance event. Micron management did not disclose specific per‑customer HBM pricing; the Street has to triangulate from NVIDIA GPU cost‑of‑goods and gross margin trajectory, which is the specific thing we won’t see cleanly until Nvidia’s Q3 FY27 print in mid‑November.

The read‑across for your book. Three pieces. First, Nvidia closed up only around 1% on the HBM4 design‑partner news that would normally rate a 3–5% session. The reason is that HBM pricing escalation is a direct ceiling on NVDA gross margin, and the Street is now pricing that compression into the FY27 forward estimate. The Anthropic $518bn compute‑commitment disclosure from last week sits as the ceiling on how much pricing power Nvidia can extract back from hyperscalers to offset the HBM cost pressure. Net: NVDA has a smaller multiple‑expansion runway than the sell‑side consensus implied a fortnight ago, and the mid‑November print is a show‑me event, not a victory lap. Second, Broadcom is modestly positive on the Micron read‑across because the custom ASIC path (Meta MTIA, Google TPU) partially bypasses the HBM cost ceiling by using HBM3E rather than HBM4E on current designs — this is a slow‑burn structural positive for AVGO on the 12–24 month view that didn’t price today. Third, AMD is net neutral: the Instinct MI400 pathway is HBM4‑dependent and takes the same cost pressure as NVIDIA, but the pricing pass‑through to Nebius / Oracle / Microsoft is cleaner than NVIDIA’s because AMD is still volume‑ramp phase. The Q3 print on 3 November is the first chance to see this measured.

The one line that didn’t get discussed enough. Micron management noted that fiscal 2027 revenue will exceed fiscal 2026, which on consensus arithmetic implies no slowdown through the memory cycle peak the Street had been expecting in calendar Q2 2027. If that holds, the AI‑memory thesis is a structural multi‑year up‑cycle rather than a one‑year spike, and the valuation framework for the whole AI stack has to be re‑rated accordingly. That is a bigger message than the FQ4 beat, and the market has not yet priced it — which is why the stock settled flat rather than up 5%. The specific thing to watch is sell‑side 2027 EPS revision moves over the next 10 sessions: if the Street takes Micron 2027 EPS from the current ~$120 handle to $140–$150, the whole semi complex has another leg to go.
