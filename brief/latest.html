02The 15 October data pair: CPI 3.7% hot, PPI 6.3%, and Warsh’s silence from Bangkok

September CPI Wednesday 3.7% headline / 2.6% core, both above consensus; PPI Thursday +6.3% YoY. The 10Y hit a 24‑year high of 5.365% intraday Wednesday before collapsing to 5.10% Thursday as the AI‑chip tape pulled money into safe‑haven duration. Warsh at IMF Bangkok Friday: “I don’t prejudge meetings”. The October FOMC on 28 October now looks like a hold at the Warsh‑era signaling bar; combined year‑end hike probability 70–80%.

The specific numbers and the specific signal

The September CPI landed Wednesday at:

  • Headline 3.7% YoY vs 3.6% consensus and 3.4% August — a 30bp acceleration and the first clean break above 3.5% since early summer. Monthly print +0.6%, driven more than 50% by gasoline (+9.1% MoM) — the direct Brent pass‑through signal.
  • Core 2.6% YoY vs 2.5% consensus and 2.4% August; monthly +0.3%. The core acceleration is modest but it is against the direction the Fed’s September dot plot had assumed.
  • Energy +6.3% YoY on the Brent/gasoline leg; shelter still around 4.1% YoY but trending lower; services ex‑shelter flat.

The Thursday PPI followed at +6.3% YoY, the largest annual gain since 2022; monthly +0.6%; final demand goods +0.8% MoM on energy. The PPI is the pipeline for the November CPI; a 6.3% YoY wholesale print with Brent at $105 says the October headline CPI (released mid‑November, around the FOMC) will not fall below 3.5% and may push 3.8%+.

Market reaction two‑step. On Wednesday the immediate hit was clean: 10Y to a 24‑year high of 5.365% intraday, 30Y to 5.733%; the dollar strengthened, equities sold off at the open; October FOMC hike probability pulled to approximately 40% at the Wednesday peak from 20% going in. By Thursday the regime shifted as the AI‑chip rotation and regional‑bank credit stress pulled money into duration: 10Y collapsed to approximately 5.10%, a three‑week low and a 23bp intraday reversal; equities bounced, dollar softened. The October hike probability settled around 27% at Thursday close; combined probability of at least one hike by December 70–80%.

Warsh at IMF Bangkok: “I don’t prejudge meetings”

Friday’s set‑piece was Fed Chair Warsh’s fireside chat at the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok. The specific lines to carry:

  • “I don’t prejudge meetings. I don’t believe in saying now what I might do at the meetings that we have in the balance of this year.”
  • “It’s a new chapter at the Fed and we’re not in that business anymore. But we can go in any direction you’d like.”
  • “At the Federal Reserve, we made very clear that we would deliver price stability, and we removed a dose of accommodation about a month ago.”

The “dose of accommodation” refers to the September FOMC action (hike or restrictive hold — not spelled out but implied as the opening move of Warsh’s chair). The “don’t prejudge” language is a deliberate departure from Powell/Williams‑era guidance: the market’s long-held habit of trading on an FOMC speaker’s mid‑cycle hint is explicitly being removed as a channel. Goldman reads this as a hold at the 28 October FOMC with the Fed watching the data; the pricing agrees. The specific framing to carry: Warsh’s Fed will not pre‑signal; the data is the only input the pricing can trade off. That is why the hot CPI landed harder than it would have under Powell/Williams — there is no soothing follow‑up speaker to pull it back.

The driver mix: the US safe‑haven bid on the AI‑chip rout (dominant), the US CPI two‑step pricing (modest headwind), and Reeves’s DMO signals ahead of the 28 October Budget (secondary tailwind). The sterling move to 1.3361 is +0.9% on last Friday’s 1.3240 — the clean reversal of three weeks of sterling weakness. FTSE 100 hit a one‑month high mid‑week on the gilt rally, which lifted UK banks (HSBC, Standard Chartered, Barclays) in sympathy; the Fidelity European sleeve catches the matching DAX move (+0.95% Friday to 25,622, highest since 17 September).

03The AI‑chip rout and its transmission to regional banks: a credit story, not an earnings one

The semiconductor complex shed approximately $1.64trn across six sessions as CoreWeave 2032 bonds traded at 77c and five‑year CDS set records on Oracle, SoftBank, META, NVDA and AMZN. On Thursday the credit stress transmitted to regional banks via two fraud disclosures: Zions −13% on a $50m California B&T charge‑off; Western Alliance −11% suing Cantor Group V for ~$100m. KRE −6%. Not SVB 2.0 — this is borrower fraud, not asset/liability mismatch — but the loan‑underwriting signal is now a second data point after Tricolor.

The specific trigger: AI capex financing stress

The AI‑chip selloff this week was not an earnings story. Nvidia unveiled the Nemotron 3.5 open‑weight models on Thursday; Jensen Huang said customers are placing AI chip orders “almost daily”; the demand picture is unchanged. What changed is the financing picture:

  • CoreWeave 2032 bonds at 77c on the dollar — the market is pricing a non‑trivial probability of default on one of the largest neoclouds. The CDS cost on CoreWeave exceeded 1,000bp this week.
  • Record five‑year CDS spreads on Oracle, SoftBank, META, NVDA and AMZN. Oracle’s senior debt now reflects the market’s uncertainty on the OpenAI‑scale datacenter capex commitments.
  • The chip‑company equity read: approximately $1.64trn erased from major chip stocks over six trading sessions — the largest six‑day drawdown in semi history outside the 2022 bear market.

The underlying question — how heavily indebted data‑center, software and semiconductor companies turn today’s capex spending into tomorrow’s profits — is the AI bubble question in its specific form: not whether demand exists (Jensen’s “almost daily” is unambiguous), but whether the financing stack can carry the capex timing. The $1,000bp CoreWeave CDS says the credit market has started pricing a scenario where some neoclouds fail to refinance into 2027–28 and the hyperscalers absorb the equipment at distressed prices. In that scenario NVDA volumes stay high but revenue per customer migrates from neoclouds to Big Tech on worse terms — which is the specific concern behind the NVDA/AMD drawdown.

The transmission to regional banks: Zions and Western Alliance

The news that landed Thursday–Friday was two bank disclosures, both borrower‑fraud driven:

  • Zions Bancorporation −13.1% on a $50m charge‑off from California Bank & Trust (its California subsidiary) on two troubled commercial loans totalling ~$60m extended to funds that invest in distressed commercial mortgages. The alleged pattern is borrower misrepresentation and contractual defaults — i.e. fraud at the borrower level, not a credit quality miss by the bank’s underwriters at the fund level.
  • Western Alliance −10.8% on a lawsuit against Cantor Group V, LLC seeking approximately $100m recovery for loan fraud. Western Alliance emphasised that total criticised assets were lower than on 30 June — i.e. the loss is a one‑off, not a trend.

SPDR S&P Regional Banking ETF (KRE) −6% by Thursday close. Jefferies sold off in sympathy. The specific thing to carry: both losses are fraud by the borrower, not deterioration in bank underwriting quality. That is the same pattern as the Tricolor subprime auto bankruptcy and the First Brands auto‑parts fraud over the summer. It is not SVB 2.0 — there is no bond‑duration mismatch, no deposit flight. But the loan‑quality signal from the real economy — borrowers failing because they lied, not because the economy is tipping — is now the second autumn data point. Markets are pricing a third shoe (hence KRE −6%); the specific candidates are the other mid‑cap commercial‑lending banks (Comerica, Columbia Banking, Pacific Premier) with distressed‑debt and auto‑parts exposure.

What it means for the Fed path and your book

The 10Y collapsed from 5.33% to 5.10% as duration caught the safe‑haven bid; big banks (JPM, GS, Citi) carried through earnings this week. On your book:

  • Direct AI‑chip hit: NVDA 2.17 shares −5.1% WoW is −£20; AMD 1.62 shares −9.8% WoW is approximately −£80; AVGO carried.
  • Look‑through through ATT (26% combined mega‑caps including 2.64% AMD + 9.19% NVDA + 8.93% GOOGL) is modestly negative — Alphabet, MSFT and META carried the week, the chip names drew it down. Approximate ATT drag: −£60 to −£100 WoW.
  • Royal London Global Equity Dividend has 2.70% NVDA + 3.00% MSFT; semi drag offset by MSFT and defensive bid.
  • SMH / RBOT directly exposed; approximately −£50 across the small positions.
  • Fundsmith was +2.9% WoW on the I Acc at 736.67p — defensive quality compounder caught the GSK‑style rotation, not the chip drag; look‑through through MSFT (large weight) also positive.
  • No direct regional‑bank exposure in your book — Zions and Western Alliance are not held and not in the trackers at material weight.

The specific thing to watch into next week on the regional bank story: whether a third mid‑cap commercial lender discloses a loan‑fraud charge‑off by 24 October. If so, KRE pushes toward −10% to −12% YTD and the regional‑bank credit story becomes a Fed input (via NFP, consumer confidence) before November. If not, the Zions/WAL stories remain isolated and the credit widening settles.
