01The five-minute version

Two things actually mattered today: the bond rout that started yesterday extended and broadened, and Xi got a red-carpet reception plus a rare-earths deal that markets priced as relief, not resolution.

One. The Treasury sell-off did not fade. The 30-year cleared a 2004 high and the 10-year printed another 19-year high.

Yesterday’s 14bp move in the 10-year through 5% was the event; today’s follow-through is the confirmation. US 30-year +6bp to 5.462%, the highest yield since 2004. US 10-year +5bp to 5.166%, another July 2007 high. The 5-year is now firmly above 5%. The drivers named in the tape were three: (i) the input-prices sub-index of yesterday’s PMI at 66.4 sinking in; (ii) building expectations of another hike from the Warsh Fed, with markets now pricing well over 90% odds by 9 December; (iii) initial jobless claims falling to 197,000 for the week ending 19 September, adding a labour-market data point that says growth is not the problem. Two consecutive multi-decade yield highs is not a positioning wobble — it is a repricing of where the terminal rate has to sit to bring a services PMI at 58.7 back to trend.

Two. Trump and Xi shook hands, extended the tariff truce to 10 January and suspended China’s rare-earth export controls. The tape called it relief, not resolution.

The state visit landed on the substantive side of the range Reuters had trailed. The tariff truce that expired 10 November was extended by two months to 10 January 2027; China agreed to suspend export controls on rare-earth materials in exchange for a reduction in US tariffs on some Chinese goods and a Chinese commitment to buy US agricultural products. The AI-titan guest list (Cook, Huang, Altman, Musk, Bezos, Dell, Fraser, Pichai) delivered the imagery Trump wanted; NPR’s read was a “cordial tone amid underlying tensions.” Notably absent from any announcement: Nvidia H20 export licensing, semiconductor equipment controls, or a Taiwan-related statement. The rare-earth suspension is a two-month reprieve; the structural US dependence (China ~90% of processing) is unchanged, which is why The Diplomat headline you would have read on your desk is “Relief Without a Resolution.”

Three. Nasdaq fell again but Meta ran the tape on Muse and Meta Connect — and Shell/BP tape decoupled from the crude fade.

Nasdaq −1.13% to 26,936, the second consecutive down session as the yield move dragged rate-sensitive tech and the SMH cluster gave back more than the mega-caps. Meta +3.8% to $777.98 broke the ranks: Meta Connect widened the AI push (agentic AI, smart glasses, commerce), and Muse continues to lead the App Store rankings a week after launch. Nvidia −1.5% to $225.51 gave back a small amount as export-control silence from the summit left the H20 question unresolved. AMD +2.4% to $628.71 with a full day range of $595.55–$630.79 — the tape stress-tested Monday’s Muse-CPU support and closed at the high, which is a real piece of information. In London, Brent gave back $4 to $97.60 (−3.9%) on the summit-driven risk-off in commodity risk premia, but Shell’s ADR was only −1.34% and BP actually printed +1.2% to 571.8p — the crude/gas cross-pressure that hurt Shell yesterday looks to have stabilised as TTF held €78/MWh, not the €72 I carried yesterday.

02The bond rout extended

Yesterday’s PMI move was the trigger. Today confirmed it was not a one-day event.

Two consecutive multi-decade yield highs, in the biggest maturities, is a specific piece of information. The Warsh Fed inherited a curve that was already priced to about a 90% probability of another 25bp hike by 9 December; a services PMI at 58.7 with input prices at 66.4 has now moved that probability higher, and the market has done the work directly rather than waiting for the SEP.

10Y +5bp to 5.166%, another July 2007 high, on top of yesterday’s 14bp. Cumulatively 19bp over two sessions is the largest two-day belly-of-the-curve sell-off since the March 2023 SVB panic in reverse. 30Y +6bp to 5.462%, the highest yield since 2004. Bloomberg described a “global bond rout” — the gilt long-end and the JGB 30-year moved in sympathy, and the axios read was that both the German bund and French OAT gave back four-day gains. The 2Y is now firmly above 4.80%, and the fact the belly is doing the work rather than the front end confirms the market read: this is not about a December hike alone, it is about the terminal being higher for longer.

The equity read was orderly, not disorderly. S&P −0.71% to 7,709.62; Nasdaq −1.13% to 26,936; Dow modestly lower. On a two-day basis the S&P is only −1.3% cumulatively and Nasdaq is roughly −2.2% — a normal absorption of an unusual bond move, not a break. The Russell 2000 tape has done the extra work as expected because small caps carry the debt burden most directly; that sell-off (~−1.5% today per the tape) is the cleanest confirmation the market is pricing higher-for-longer rather than a growth event.

Labour data added a piece. Initial jobless claims for the week ending 19 September fell to 197,000 from 198,000 revised, the four-week average down to 202,250. There is no crack in the labour market that the Fed would need to see before green-lighting further tightening. Combined with yesterday’s services PMI 58.7, the growth-side of the dual mandate is not going to argue against another hike; the entire case rests on inflation, which is exactly what tomorrow’s US August core PCE speaks to.

What tomorrow’s PCE settlesConsensus core PCE month-on-month is ~0.3%. An in-line print keeps December pricing intact and the belly of the curve consolidates around 5.15%; a hot print (0.4% or higher) confirms the input-prices sub-index of yesterday’s PMI has already leaked into the Fed’s preferred gauge, and the 10Y probably targets 5.25% next. A soft print (0.2%) unwinds part of the two-day move but does not reverse it. The event risk on your book runs through UKW £9,400 (long-end gilt spread) and through the ~£93,000 dollar look-through (further FX pressure). Even in the hot-print scenario, cumulative losses to the mark are probably bounded at ~£500–700, which is not a size that changes strategic positioning.

03The Trump–Xi summit, and what the tape did with it

A rare-earth deal, a truce extension, and a state dinner. What was not agreed was more informative than what was.

The substantive deliverables landed roughly where Reuters had trailed. The tariff truce that had been set to expire 10 November was extended by two months to 10 January 2027. China agreed to suspend export controls on rare-earth minerals in exchange for a reduction of some US tariffs on Chinese goods and a Chinese commitment to buy US agricultural products. Trump’s framing was that the meeting was “great” and that a rare-earths deal was “done”; Xi’s side described the tone as cordial and constructive but avoided declaring resolution of any structural issues. The AI-titan guest list at the state dinner (Cook, Huang, Altman, Musk, Bezos, Pichai, Dell, Fraser) provided the visual the White House wanted — the message that US tech is aligned with the administration’s China posture.

What was not delivered is what makes this a relief trade, not a resolution. There was no announcement on Nvidia H20 export licensing to Chinese customers, no movement on semiconductor equipment controls, no Taiwan-related statement, no framework for AI incident notification (which Bessent had trailed on Sunday). The rare-earth suspension is a two-month window, not a structural change; China’s ~90% share of global processing is unchanged, and the leverage that produced today’s concession is intact for the next round. The Diplomat’s take — “Relief Without a Resolution” — is the frame your desk should treat as canonical for how this affects the industrials cluster, the auto OEMs and the defence primes.

The tape read was cleaner than the political read. Commodity risk premia came out fast: Brent −3.9% to $97.60, WTI to ~$92.86, and the trade-sensitive parts of the mining and industrials complex firmed while the H20-sensitive names (Nvidia in particular, −1.5%) gave back. DiDi and EMQQ almost certainly traded the relief but I could not verify the closes tonight. The lack of a China ADR delisting mention on the published agenda, which I flagged last night, was borne out — there was nothing on the topic in the readouts, which is a small removal of tail risk on your £1,446 DiDi and £214 EMQQ positions.

The specific read to your look-through book: rare-earths relief is a positive for Fidelity European £15,611 (Schneider, ASML supply chains use rare earth magnets in servos and motors), a small positive for Meta £2,300 look-through (data-centre cooling and networking gear), and a marginal positive for Berkshire’s Precision Castparts business inside BRK-B £6,177. Set against that: no H20 progress is a marginal negative for Nvidia £5,300 look-through, though the impact is small because the market had not priced any specific movement on the topic.

The interesting non-eventThe Trump–Xi state dinner has been described in most tape reports as the AI event of the quarter, but the concrete AI outcomes were nil. The dinner guest list looked more like a signal to Beijing that US tech is on-side with the administration than a working session, and the summit day produced no new export-control framework, no AI incident notification protocol, and no H20 licensing update. If the AI-China question is central to the tape’s read of the semiconductor complex — and it is — the summit ended it exactly where it began. That is worth putting in the diary because it means the next real catalyst is either the December FOMC (mechanical) or the CY26 hyperscaler capex guides on 28 October (fundamental).
