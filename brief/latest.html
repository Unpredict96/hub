04The Qatar LNG tanker attack and the TTF paradox

The specific contradiction on tonight’s tape: the oil complex sold off hard on the Doha peace draft while European gas was flat‑to‑weaker on the same news despite a weekend LNG tanker attack that broke the Iranian clearance protocol. TTF settled approximately €102/MWh −1.87% day — a much more muted move than oil’s −3.6%.

The Al Mayeda incident

  • Marshall Islands‑flagged Al Mayeda, operated by Qatar Gas Transport Company (Nakilat), was struck by unidentified objects while passing through Gulf waters on Friday evening 30 October. The vessel was transporting LNG from Qatar to Pakistan.
  • The specific point that matters most: Pakistan had obtained prior permission from Iran for the vessel’s safe passage hours before the attack. Indian Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal confirmed the vessel had been targeted in a projectile attack. The Iranian clearance protocol — which has functioned as the de facto safe‑passage mechanism for Pakistan’s cargoes since the war opened — was specifically broken.
  • All 25 crew (including 6 Indians) safe. The vessel turned back into the Persian Gulf per ship‑tracking data. The LNG cargo remained intact. Qatar’s Foreign Ministry opened an investigation; Gulf countries including Saudi Arabia, UAE and Oman condemned the attack.
  • The specific implication for the Qatari LNG supply chain: the Iranian clearance channel can no longer be assumed to confer safety. Qatari cargoes to Pakistan, China, Taiwan, South Korea and Japan all depend on Strait of Hormuz transit; the Al Mayeda incident puts a specific risk premium on all Qatari LNG flows regardless of destination.
  • Qatar has force majeure extended through end‑December 2026 for Edison, Pakistan and Bangladesh cargoes (per last week’s read); the Al Mayeda incident specifically extends that FM read to include “previously‑cleared” Pakistan cargoes.

Additional weekend strikes

  • Saturday 1 November: the Panama‑flagged tug Selat Storm, UAE‑owned, was struck by an unidentified projectile at approximately 15:00 UTC and reported partially sinking; a tanker 72nm east of Ras Al Khafji was struck by two unknown projectiles on its port side (crew accounted for, no environmental impact).
  • Sunday 2 November evening: IRGC Navy claimed two oil tankers were struck by mines after “crossing the Strait of Hormuz without Iranian authorization” — one described as a supertanker. The specific wording from IRGC positions this as retaliation for unauthorised transit rather than attacks on cleared cargoes.
  • The pattern: the Al Mayeda strike broke the clearance protocol for Pakistan; the Sunday night IRGC strikes reinforced the protocol for everyone else. The specific contradiction: Iran is simultaneously negotiating in Doha, striking cleared cargoes, and strike‑claiming uncleared ones.

Why TTF held firm despite oil’s rout

  • Oil is pricing the peace draft. Gas is pricing storage anxiety plus LNG supply disruption. The two tapes are structurally decoupling at the current moment.
  • EU aggregate storage 74.6% on 31 October per AGSI+ — essentially unchanged from Friday’s 74.9% read. Still 14 points below 5‑year average.
  • Germany at 61.5% on 1 November — the emptiest start to any winter since GIE records began in 2011. This is the specific tape line that holds TTF firm. The Energy Council in early November will be the political forum where Germany makes its storage‑flexibility ask.
  • Italy 91.1%, Poland 99.2%, Portugal 95.5% — the specific country dispersion. The Mediterranean and Baltic states are substantially above target; Germany is substantially below. The political implication: Germany’s ask for the 4% waiver on the 80% target is specifically harder to secure because other member states have overshot. The Energy Council forum in early November is where the ask lands.
  • Net read: TTF −1.87% to approximately €102/MWh is a muted move; the peace tape only compressed about half of the premium that the oil tape surrendered. The specific implication: the gas market is pricing a longer tail for the physical disruption than the oil market is.

Transmission to the Shell three‑leg thesis

The Al Mayeda tanker attack and the Sunday mine strikes specifically protect leg 1 and leg 3 of the three‑leg thesis. Leg 1 (TTF >€60) is specifically protected because the Qatari LNG supply chain risk premium just repriced on the Al Mayeda tape; Shell’s Q4 Integrated Gas realised price at the current TTF strip still models at approximately $8.80/mscf.
