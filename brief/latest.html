02Oil faded on the Trump midterms line and the equity tape reversed — but the physical story in Hormuz did not change

Brent −1.6% to $102.60 on a Trump reporter line that the US would not strike Iran before the 3 November midterms. IRGC continued striking tankers through the morning. The equity tape treated the headline as the ceiling to escalation; the physical oil balance did not change. Shell held, BP caught up, TTF faded 3%.

The specific trigger and the specific limit of it

The oil fade was headline‑driven, not fundamental. Brent settled Friday around $102.60/bbl, down from Thursday’s $103.76 close and well below the overnight $106–$107 range. The trigger landed in the US morning: President Trump told reporters on the margins of a WH event that the US would not launch an attack on Iran before the 3 November midterms. For a market that had added approximately $5–7/bbl of political risk premium through the week on the Hormuz framework collapse, the Trump line was the signal to pull some of it back. The specific point to carry: this is a political ceiling, not a physical floor. Trump pulling the US off a 25‑day escalation path reduces the probability of a direct US–Iran kinetic exchange within that window, but it changes none of (i) Hormuz tanker traffic (still at a 2‑month low per CNBC counts; 11 attacks in the week to 4 October, 5 more by Monday); (ii) Iranian IRGC operational tempo (overnight into Friday they struck the LPG carrier NV Sunshine south of the Strait, engine room and propulsion fire reported); (iii) Qatar LNG force majeure extension (confirmed through end-November for Pakistan/Bangladesh, early December for Edison); (iv) Russian refining damage. If the market retests the $100 level on this political framing, the physical signal is still Brent $103 as the sustaining price for the fundamental trade.

Equity transmission: tech led the bounce, energy held but did not add

The S&P 500 closed +0.59% to 7,811.51; Nasdaq +0.64% to 27,366.17; Dow +0.83% to approximately 51,655. Reversal of Thursday was the specific pattern: cost‑shock‑sensitive sectors (transports, consumer discretionary, chip complex) led the bounce; defensives sold on the rotation back into risk. Among the lines: Shell held at 3,793.50p (+0.3%) — the London close locked in before the Trump line pulled crude lower in the US afternoon, which is the specific reason the Thursday leg carried through. BP played catch‑up at 584.80p (+2.8%) — the sector read Shell got Thursday finally landed on BP, partially closing the Shell‑over‑BP spread that had widened Thursday. GSK sold −2.5% to 1,735.50p as the risk‑on rotation pulled yield‑proxy defensives lower — the same dynamic as the Monday 5 October session when Friday’s dovish NFP triggered a similar −0.86% in GSK, and the pattern the 3 October weekly review called out specifically.

The three defensive legs on Shell: none broke today, one weakened at the margin

The Thursday brief laid out the three legs that have to break to re‑open the Shell trim conversation. The Friday session’s test of each:

  • LNG decoupling. TTF closed €76.48/MWh, −3.01% on Thursday’s €79.85 — first down day in four sessions as the oil fade pulled through the oil‑indexed LNG curve. TTF above €60 is the breaking level; today’s €76 is comfortably clear, and the Qatar FM extension through end‑November for Pakistan/Bangladesh and early December for Edison is the structural anchor underneath. Leg intact.
  • Diesel crack. The specific physical signal is whether Delta’s Q3 fuel pass‑through corroborates the $42/bbl Shell refining margin. It did: Delta absorbed $1.6bn of fuel costs on $20.19bn revenue, cut FY, and said fares are rising to recover fuel. That is the pass‑through side of the thesis working in real time. The G7 100mb release is in week two; Chinese fuel export suspension still not restarted (now nine days past the 7 October window). Leg intact.
  • $100 floor for Brent. The specific question is whether today’s fade signals $100 was a ceiling after all. The physical case: Hormuz traffic at a 2‑month low; IRGC striking tankers daily; Qatar FM structural; OPEC+ steady. The political case: Trump pulling US off escalation path for 25 days. If the market gets below $100 on the political framing, the physical case will support it. Leg intact but weakened at the margin — the specific lesson is that any Trump statement from here that extends the no‑attack framework beyond midterms would be the specific catalyst that breaks this leg.

Net: today the oil fade was priced as a political relief rally, not a physical reset. The Shell position marks essentially flat; the Q3 formal print on 29 October remains the specific anchor and today’s Delta read is incremental support for the Q4 margin trajectory. Hold stands.

The specific risk framing to carry into next weekIf Trump makes any statement in the next 48 hours that extends the no‑attack framework beyond 3 November — e.g. tying it to a specific diplomatic track or framework agreement — the oil fade accelerates and the Shell three‑legged thesis loses its political-overlay anchor. Watch Fed and WH comms through the weekend. If the no‑attack line stays specifically midterm‑dated, the physical story reasserts into next week as the IRGC tempo and Qatar FM continue.

03Michigan preliminary at 46.3 with 1Y inflation expectations 4.7% — the stagflation needle is now explicit

The 15:00 BST release missed hard. Sentiment 46.3 vs 47.8 consensus and 48.1 September. 1Y inflation expectations ticked to 4.7% from 4.6%. That is the specific combination Williams “may be appropriate / no urgency” was positioned against, and the CPI print on 15 October is now the single most important data point remaining before November FOMC.

The specific numbers and the specific signal

The preliminary University of Michigan consumer sentiment release landed at 46.3 against consensus 47.8 and September’s 48.1. A 3.7‑point drop, the second lowest print of the cycle, and the hardest single‑month decline since the April tariff shock. 1‑year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6%; 5‑10 year expectations held at 3.8%. Current conditions index fell 2.8 points; the expectations index fell 4.5 points. The specific framing: consumer psychology is accelerating on inflation at exactly the moment labour‑market softness is anchoring the dovish case. That is the textbook stagflation combination.

For FOMC positioning this is the hardest pin on Williams’ Thursday language yet. “May be appropriate” is now anchored by the specific inflation‑expectations lift, and “no urgency” is harder to defend because the second‑order inflation signal is accelerating against the first‑order one. The implication is that the CPI print on Wednesday 15 October becomes the single data point that locks November FOMC:

  • Core CPI ≤ 3.0% YoY → outright skip remains the base case for November; Williams “no urgency” defends. December hike then is the implementation window.
  • Core CPI 3.0–3.1% YoY → November probability pushes back to the 60–75% range; the market reads the combination of today’s inflation expectations + CPI confirmation as sufficient grounds for Williams “may be appropriate” to translate to action.
  • Core CPI ≥ 3.2% YoY → November hike close to certain. The two‑sided framing collapses; the terminal rate pricing pushes to 4.75% minimum, possibly 5.00% through late 2027.

Market re‑pricing today was two‑sided and modest

The near end bull‑steepened on the sentiment miss, bear‑flattened on the inflation expectations. Net, the US 10Y moved to around 5.19–5.22% at the close, down ~6bp from Thursday’s 5.27%; 2Y around 4.85%, down ~5bp from 4.90%; 30Y around 5.58%, down ~7bp from 5.65%. The curve steepened mildly in bull form. FOMC pricing: October probability stuck around 17–20%, no material change on the day; November probability pulled to around 55–65%, down from Thursday’s 65–72% as the softer sentiment print bought Williams a little more cover for “no urgency”; December probability held around 85–90%. The 2027 terminal rate stayed around 4.50–4.75% — still no re‑pricing of the destination, only the implementation.

UK gilts rallied with the US; the FTSE caught the double tailwind

UK 30Y gilt yield eased from Thursday’s 6.05% intraday peak to around 5.93% at the close — the first clean day below 6% since the gilt break triggered Tuesday. The move was dollar‑rate‑driven not gilt‑story‑driven: no change in DMO guidance, no Budget signal, no Treasury intervention. UK 10Y followed to around 5.43%. Sterling rallied in sympathy to GBP/USD 1.3240, +0.4% — snapping the three‑day down‑sequence as both the dollar softened and the UK duration repair bought back some of the fiscal‑premium wedge. The FTSE 100 closed 10,552, +1.1% as the gilt rally and oil fade both relieved pressure on banks (HSBC, Standard Chartered); the Shell/BP contribution kept the energy anchor in place.

The specific thing to watch into next week on gilts: whether this is a one‑day retracement or the start of a sustained compression of the 30Y fiscal premium. The Budget on Tuesday 28 October remains the specific operational event. If Reeves delivers a short‑end biased DMO issuance schedule, the 30Y can push to 5.75% as the premium compresses; if the Budget falls short on fiscal credibility, the 30Y back-tests 6% within a session.

04Delta Q3 and Pepsi Q3: both validated the pass‑through theme, both cut guidance

Delta: $1.72 EPS miss on fuel but revenue +16% to $20.19bn; absorbed $1.6bn fuel; cut FY. Pepsi: $2.34 EPS beat; revenue $25.27bn; cut FY organic EPS guide to +2.5–3.5% from +5–7%. Together: top-line pass‑through working, margins compressing. The specific read for Shell: the pass‑through side of the $42/bbl refining margin is anchored by the Delta pricing move.

Delta: the specific validation of Shell’s refining thesis

Delta Air Lines Q3 pre‑market print landed as the clean cross‑sector read Thursday’s brief flagged. The numbers:

  • EPS $1.72 against consensus $1.76–$1.88 (midpoint $1.82). Miss of ~5.5% on the midpoint.
  • Revenue $20.19bn, +16% YoY, beating the $18.83bn consensus by $1.27bn — the largest Q3 revenue beat in Delta’s recent history.
  • Premium revenue $6.82bn (+18% YoY); main cabin $6.8bn (+12% YoY). Operating margin 9.4%. Pre‑tax profit $1.5bn.
  • $1.6bn absorbed in higher Q3 fuel costs — the specific line that gates the EPS miss.
  • 2026 FY profit outlook cut; Bastian said demand remains strong, fares are rising further as fuel stays high. The pricing power is intact; the margin is where the oil story is landing.

For Shell, the read is exact. The $42/bbl indicative Q3 refining margin Shell printed Wednesday depends on two things: (i) the physical crack spreads (diesel, gasoline) staying firm, and (ii) the customer side absorbing the pass‑through so that volumes do not collapse. Delta today validated (ii): $20.19bn of revenue on +18% premium growth is a customer that is absorbing fuel passthrough through fare increases. If Delta can do it with 55m seats and a cyclical demand pattern, Shell’s refining customers (industrial diesel, marine fuel, aviation jet) are structurally better positioned to absorb. The $42/bbl margin print survives contact with the real-economy fuel bill, which is the single most important point for the 29 October Shell formal print trajectory.

PepsiCo: top‑line pricing working, margin outlook cut

Pepsi Q3 landed before Delta with the specific framing: pricing is landing, volumes are modestly recovering, FY margin outlook cut. The numbers:

  • EPS $2.34 against consensus $2.29 — modest beat.
  • Revenue $25.27bn, +5.6% YoY; organic growth 3.1%; international organic +8%, North America mixed (snacks volume +low-single-digit after pricing reset, beverages soft).
  • FY core EPS guide cut to +2.5–3.5% from +5–7% — this is the specific pass‑through-is-hurting-margins signal.
  • Revenue guide upgraded to the top of the prior 4–6% range — pricing is landing.

The combined read across Pepsi and Delta: the US consumer is absorbing pricing through Q4, which supports the Fed stagflation reading (the Michigan inflation‑expectations lift is being validated by real pricing landing) and anchors the November‑skip‑December‑hike sequence. For the fund sleeve this is the specific read: consumer staples hold, consumer discretionary (which has to compete with pricing-through) will under‑perform. The Royal London, Fundsmith and tracker sleeves have modest consumer discretionary exposure through Nike, Starbucks and the like; the specific weighting is immaterial at this bookmark but the direction is worth noting.
