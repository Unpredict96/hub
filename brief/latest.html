02 Nvidia’s quarter, taken apart

The quarter was strong. The headline Q4 guide overstates it a little. The governance overhang is now the reason the stock did not react more, and the call is where Kress either reduces that overhang or adds to it.

The extra week and China

The $130bn guide is the number on every headline. The release states that Q4 contains an extra week, so it is a 14-week quarter. Scaled back to 13 weeks, the guide is about $120.7bn, which is +4.2% on Q3’s $115.8bn rather than the +12% the headline implies. China cuts the other way. One preview put Q3’s China Hopper shipments at about $2.4bn. I found that figure in one source only, and the release itself does not break it out. If it is right, Q3 excluding China was about $113.4bn, and like-for-like growth from there to a 13-week, zero-China Q4 is about +6.5%. Either way, the like-for-like guide still points up, but growth is slowing from +20% q/q this quarter. That matters for what the market pays for the stock, and it is probably the main reason a $7.8bn beat produced a 2–3% move rather than 8–10%.

Gross margin

The fall from 74.9% to 71–72% looks bad on a scorecard (24/7 Wall St gave margins a C+), but it was announced in advance. In August Kress said margins would bottom in Q4 at 71–72% while Vera Rubin ramps, then recover to 72–73% as Nvidia’s own price increases come through. Rubin is now about 20% of data-centre revenue, which coverage calls the fastest architecture ramp in the company’s history. New architectures carry lower margins early in their ramp, so the dip is in line with Rubin selling well. The call needs to confirm the 72–73% recovery path. If Kress pushes it out a quarter, the margin story changes from a planned dip to a lasting step down.

I am setting out the FY28 test now, before the call, so that the after-hours move does not decide it for you. It fires if Kress says the roughly 70% figure no longer holds, or replaces it with a number below about 50%, or says the company will “no longer provide” multi-year framing. It does not fire if she reaffirms the figure, declines to update it until a later event without walking it back, or describes FY28 as “supply-constrained” in the same terms as August. If it fires, sell the line in Wednesday’s US session. Do not sell into the after-hours book, where spreads are wide and moves on a call tend to overshoot.

Governance got louder today

The release itself was clean. The news around it was not. During the day, Huang said he would be “honored” to return as CEO (Yahoo, 16:36 UTC). A petition for his return passed 180,000 signatures. People familiar with the board say it is “not looking to bring Huang back”. Bessent wants an outside CEO, and Trump has said he backs Huang. Dan Ives notes that calling a special meeting needs holders of 15% of the shares for a year, so the petition has no direct route to the board. Huang appears before the House Foreign Affairs Committee on 1 December, and Nvidia’s general counsel on 2 December. The board has set up a committee on export-control compliance. CNBC reported that Nvidia has warned customers about legal risk over Chinese access to its chips, and that it will not sell to customers who rent more than 50% of their GPUs to Chinese companies. That is the commercial effect of Monday’s BIS rule.

The new item is credit. The WSJ reported, citing JPMorgan, that Nvidia’s lending exposure is about $530bn including guarantees. Bloomberg reported that the $500bn lending platform has not closed a deal, because lenders want Nvidia to back more than 25% of each loan, and the WSJ expects Kress to tighten financing further. Dan Niles’s view is that the “Bank of Nvidia” will be “slammed shut” and that this hurts the neoclouds. This has a direct read-across to the revenue line. If part of Nvidia’s demand comes from customers whose purchases it helps finance, tighter financing slows that demand. I cannot size that share from public data. The question for the call is whether Kress quantifies the vendor-financed share of AI-cloud revenue, the $47.5bn bucket. Separately, Soros’s family office cut its NVDA stake by 20% in Q3. That is old news by filing date and not a signal for tonight.

Read-across to the rest of the sleeve

On Monday I argued that a clean guide would help ASML and TSM more than NVDA, because their discount reflects the capex cycle while NVDA’s is partly about governance. Tonight’s numbers support that. A $107.6bn data-centre quarter, Rubin at 20% of the mix and a guide pointing up are good news for capex, and nothing in them concerns the CEO. I could not verify after-hours moves for TSM or ASML, so I don’t know yet whether the market sees it the same way. TSM has fallen 13.0% in four sessions, from $413.35 to $359.69, which is the steepest fall in the sleeve. Wednesday’s session will show whether it recovers or whether the market keeps selling the suppliers even after a strong print. A gap up that fades by the close would be the more worrying outcome.

03 Oil: Qeshm puts a floor under the slide

Monday settled closer to Shell’s trigger than any day since the thesis was set. On Tuesday the US struck Iranian territory for the first time in this run of incidents, and Brent rose about $2.

The tape

Monday 16 November: Brent settled at $92.44, −3.6%, per one source. That is in line with Monday’s brief, which estimated about $92.3 from a late Yahoo print. I take it as the settle, from a single source. On that figure, Monday’s settle was $2.44 above Shell’s trigger, the narrowest gap at any settle since the rule was set.

Tuesday 17 November: Brent was down about 0.5% near $92 before Europe opened, with a reported low of $91.66. It then recovered. FXEmpire’s ticker had Brent at $94.92 (+1.76%) and WTI at $86.61 (+1.31%) at 19:08 GMT, and OilPrice’s ticker had about $92.83 at an unknown time. I have no settle. My best read is $93–94, so Brent is about $3–5 above the trigger tonight.

Why it turned

Qeshm. The IRGC claimed a third drone attack in under a week on US radar in Bahrain, this time at Sheikh Isa Air Base. CENTCOM called the claim of a hit “FALSE” and said all the drones were intercepted. By 14:38 ET a US military official confirmed precision strikes on an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island, “in self-defense”. Qeshm sits inside the Strait. Until now the war at sea has been mines and drones against ships and radar, and the US has struck back at Iranian territory only rarely. Qatar, which is mediating, said talks continue and that a meeting with the Iranian side was planned for Tuesday. It also warned that escalation is an “extremely grave danger” to a deal. Trump spent the day warning about a reported Iranian dirty-bomb plot. Iran and Oman held separate talks on Hormuz security in Muscat.

Mines, still. The IRGC claimed three more tanker mine strikes on Tuesday, and UKMTO reported a disabled tanker north of Khasab on Monday. The US says the southern lanes have been cleared, and the IRGC says they are “fully mined”. These claims have not moved prices on their own for three sessions. Monday’s point still stands: the market trades the flows, and the flows are recovering.

More supply. Iraq restarted the Kirkuk–Fishkhabur pipeline to Turkey, idle since 2014, at 120 kb/d with a target of 500 kb/d. That route avoids Hormuz. Iraqi output is reported at its highest since 2019. Chinese independent refiners are paying $6–9 over Brent for Iraqi and UAE crude, a sign that prompt Gulf barrels are still short in Asia even as flows recover. China’s implied crude stock build of 492 kb/d in October is unchanged from Monday. OPEC has started a capacity audit with DeGolyer & MacNaughton, aiming for more realistic quotas from 2027. That is a 2027 issue, but it points to more barrels, not fewer.

05 Gas and LNG desk view

The weather drove Monday’s 7% fall and the forecast cold drove Tuesday’s bounce. Storage is the lowest for the date on record. Corrected for the exchange rate, Asia pays more than Monday’s brief said. NBP has moved to a small premium over TTF.

Prices

TTF front-month: Friday closed at about €90.2–90.8. On Monday it fell about 7–7.5% to roughly €84/MWh (Trading Economics via Serbia-Energy). Bloomberg had the intraday fall at 6.2%. Reports put it down to milder weather this week and record weekend feedgas to US LNG export plants. Tuesday recovered: €85.37 (+€0.62) at 08:44 GMT, €85.85 (+1.41%) at 10:12 GMT, and about €86 (+2%) early in Amsterdam. I have no Tuesday settle. Monday’s level was the lowest since the 6 November trough of €85.59 and is about 16% below the late-October peak above €100. A year ago TTF was about €30.

NBP: front-month 213.88p/therm (+1.31%) at 10:12 GMT. The last day-ahead settle I have is 207.00p on 12 November. A broker sheet on 16 November had Q1-27 at about 222p and Summer-27 at about 163p. That is a ~59p winter–summer backwardation, which tells you the market sees this winter as the tight period and expects relief by summer.

TTF against NBP. Converting €85.85/MWh at GBP/EUR 1.1996 gives about 209.7p/therm, so NBP was about 4p (2%) above TTF on Tuesday morning. Last Thursday the two were roughly level. The likely reason is UK-specific Norwegian supply. On 12 November Gassco showed Troll curtailments of 11.3 mcm/d and an unplanned Åsgard outage of 6.5 mcm/d, which cut flows to Britain to about 110 mcm/d. If those outages are still running, the UK is short of pipeline gas at the margin. Its storage cannot cover the gap the way the continent’s can, so it has to draw LNG. I have not verified that the outages are still in place.

JKM: no fresh print. The last I have is $31.35/MMBtu, carried over from Monday and not re-verified. At the corrected EUR/USD of about 1.10, TTF at €85.85 is about $27.7/MMBtu. If JKM has held, Asia is paying about $3.6/MMBtu more than Europe, not the $1 Monday’s brief calculated at 1.15. A $3–4 spread is more than enough to divert flexible US cargoes to Asia. That makes the record US feedgas Monday’s market relied on less useful to Europe than it looks. It also makes Monday’s 7% fall harder to justify on fundamentals.

UK power: Winter 26/27 baseload not verified.

Storage

EU aggregate: 70.4% (17 November, Yahoo/Reuters). Other trackers have 70.6–71.0%. That is about 19 points below the five-year average of roughly 89–90%, and about 12 points below a year ago. Net withdrawals have started in almost every EU country.

Germany: “below 58%” (FXLeaders). The last dated figure I have is 57.5% on 13 November. Germany’s storage association INES warned on Tuesday that “gas supply in Germany may not be fully secured” in extreme cold. That is the most important new line in this section. A milder week slows the 0.3-points-a-day draw I estimated on Monday. The cold forecast from about 20 November into early December speeds it up again.

Italy: about 91% at last check, not re-verified. UK: aggregate about 40% and Rough at 0.0 TWh are both from last week and not re-verified.

Supply

Qatar: force majeure on deliveries extended to at least the end of December. About 17% of export capacity is damaged, with repairs to two trains taking about three years. LNG cargoes leaving Hormuz are estimated at about one-third of pre-war levels (Bloomberg/OilPrice). Crude flows have nearly recovered, and LNG flows have not. That gap is today’s most important supply observation for gas, because it means the recovery that is pulling Brent down is not reaching TTF. I have no buyer-by-buyer cargo counts. Qatar’s mediation meeting with Iran on Tuesday is the restart route for the undamaged trains. The Qeshm strikes make that harder.

Norway: Troll at 11.3 mcm/d curtailed and Åsgard at 6.5 mcm/d unplanned, both as of 12 November. Still no return date for Visund after the 4–5 November fire. I have no current total NCS nominations. Dated returns for Troll and Åsgard are the two most likely bearish catalysts for NBP specifically.

UK LNG sendout: about 47 mcm/d from South Hook, Dragon and Isle of Grain on 12 November, down from an expected 64 mcm/d on 6 November. Not re-verified. If sendout has stayed in the 40s while Norway is short, NBP’s premium to TTF has room to widen.

US: record feedgas over the weekend. Henry Hub front-month was about $3.10 (+2.7%) on Tuesday evening.

Freight: not verified.

Policy

Nothing new from the Commission or the IEA today. The EU Energy Council in the week of 1 December still has Germany’s request for 4% storage flexibility on its agenda. After today’s INES warning, that request is more likely to get a hearing. The UK WCfD consultation closes on 9 December.
