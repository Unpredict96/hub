02Meta priced: $625 strike, below‑strike close, cornerstones named

The specific transmission from Monday’s announcement to Tuesday’s pricing. The size went up, the strike landed at the midpoint of the price‑talk range, the cornerstones are named and smaller than the Monday upper bound suggested, and the stock closed below the Class A strike by $4.81. All four of those details sharpen the thesis.

The specific pricing terms

  • Class A common: $25bn at $625 per share, 40 million shares. This is upsized from the $22.5bn originally announced Monday morning. The strike sits at approximately a 0.9% discount to Monday’s close of $630.56 — well below the typical 4–7% discount for a $25bn concurrent raise, which is partly explained by the cornerstone backstop. The common was conducted in connection with forward sale agreements, meaning Meta does not receive all the cash immediately; the full receipt is spread as the hedge is unwound by the dealers.
  • Convertible preferred: $12.5bn, 250m depositary shares at $50 each, 6.5% coupon. The 6.5% is the specific number the market will carry: on a $12.5bn book, that is $812.5m of annual preferred dividend obligations until the ~15 August 2029 mandatory conversion. The conversion mechanic lets Meta choose between Class A or Class C non‑voting (which starts trading Q1 2027) — the structural detail that enables the Class C ATM programme to run against the preferred conversion schedule.
  • Combined priced size: $37.5bn, not the $35bn originally announced. The $2.5bn upsize is the specific signal that the demand book was oversubscribed, which is consistent with the small discount to Monday’s close.
  • Lead underwriters: Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs. The specific pair that normally runs the big tech ECM books.
  • The upper bound: up to $97.5bn. The priced $37.5bn plus up to $60bn from the Class C ATM programme once Class C starts trading Q1 2027. The $97.5bn is roughly 70% of a single year’s raised FY26 capex guide ($135–145bn), and the implicit admission that the full capex envelope requires equity funding across five quarters, not just one.

The cornerstones — named and sized

The Monday framing was “Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom indicated interest in buying Class A for up to $5bn in total”. The Tuesday pricing filings now say:

  • Nvidia: $3bn. The largest cornerstone commitment. The specific signal: Nvidia is funding the largest hyperscaler capex customer at the equity layer with cash that will come back as revenue over the next 24 months. This is the reflex loop in physical form.
  • AMD: $1bn. Smaller in absolute size but the bigger surprise relative to AMD’s balance sheet. AMD’s FY26 operating cash flow runs approximately $8–9bn; committing $1bn into Meta’s equity means ~12% of a year’s cash earmarked for a hyperscaler’s balance sheet rather than AMD’s own buyback or MI400 capex. That is a specific statement about how much AMD values the Meta MI450 pipeline. The AMD stock reaction (+5.59%) reflects the specific market read that AMD’s share of the Meta capex is being validated at a higher base case than previously priced.
  • Broadcom: not confirmed in the pricing filings I reached. Monday’s “up to $5bn combined” language suggested Broadcom at $1bn as the implicit third cornerstone, but the Tuesday pricing confirmed only Nvidia and AMD. That gap — whether AVGO sits out or participates smaller — is the specific data point the Wednesday Q3 reads should settle.

What the close below strike means

META closed $620.19 on Tuesday, which is $4.81 below the $625 Class A strike. The specific framing: when an equity issue closes below the strike on the pricing day, the market is saying the deal priced at the midpoint of the price‑talk range rather than at a premium, and the forward‑sale economics now sit against the strike rather than through it. Three reads of why that number landed where it did:

  • The upsize was the specific response to oversubscription at the midpoint. If demand had been at the high end, the strike would have been pushed through $630 and the stock would have closed above $625. The upsize suggests demand was deep enough to add $2.5bn of common at a slight discount but not deep enough to push through the Monday close.
  • The hedge supply from the forward sale agreements is now live. Underwriters typically short the stock to hedge the forward sale, which creates specific selling pressure into the pricing window. The Monday–Tuesday 2.3% drag (from $630.56 to $620.19) is consistent with that mechanical pressure.
  • The absence of a Broadcom cornerstone trimmed the specific sentiment lift. If AVGO had printed a $1bn commitment Tuesday, the stock would likely have held the $625 strike. The reads from the Wednesday Q3 call on Broadcom’s posture toward Meta ASIC supply is now the specific missing data point.
