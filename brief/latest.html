02ISM services prices‑paid at 74.0 broke the dovish NFP read — then Tuesday’s risk‑on took the long end back lower

Monday’s services print was the first signal that the labour slowdown has not pulled services pricing with it. The curve pushed the long end to fresh 2002 highs. Tuesday reversed cleanly on multiple‑expansion risk‑on into two record closes.

The Monday release. The ISM Services PMI landed at 54.9 vs 55 consensus, down from 55.4 in August. On its own, a mild deceleration consistent with trend. The sub‑components tell the story: Business Activity 56.5, New Orders 59.8 (a 10‑month high), Supplier Deliveries 53.2, Employment 50.1 (back above the 50 breakeven line). The single line that broke the Fed debate was Prices Paid at 74.0, the highest since July 2022 and up from 69.2 in August — a 4.8‑point acceleration in a single month on top of two prior monthly gains. Services inflation does not have the tariff or energy pass‑through excuse that goods pricing has leaned on; a 74.0 print reads as broad‑based input‑cost acceleration in the largest segment of the US economy. The composition — growth 54.9, pricing 74.0 — is the pure stagflation signature. Thursday’s manufacturing ISM at 54.5 plus Friday’s NFP at +29k plus Monday’s services pricing at 74.0 is the three‑data‑point sequence that forces the Fed to defend both legs of the dual mandate from opposite sides simultaneously.

The Monday curve reaction. The long end broke higher cleanly. US 10Y closed 5.31%, the highest in 24 years; 30Y to 5.67%, 20Y to 5.72%, both fresh 2026 highs; 7Y to 5.19%. The 2Y actually held near 4.90% — the market distinguished between the near‑end dovish repricing (October skip survives, December pricing softens on prices‑paid versus labour) and the long‑end stagflation re‑pricing (term premium rising on inflation‑compensation demand). The 2s10s spread widened a further ~5bp on Monday, extending the Friday steepener. The specific line to carry from Monday: the bull steepener Friday turned into a bear steepener Monday — same curve shape, opposite drivers. That is the sign that the term‑premium story has broken through the near‑term inflation hedge story. In GSK’s book this showed up as a 0.86% drop Friday reversing to +1.19% Tuesday — the yield‑proxy trade got sold on the near end move, bought on the long‑end reversal.

Tuesday’s reversal. Clean. 10Y to 5.27%, −4bp. 30Y to 5.63%, −4bp. 20Y to 5.68%, −4bp. Multiple expansion took over: the S&P closed 7,819.04, +0.58%, first record close since early August; Nasdaq 27,599.79, +0.45%, also a record; Dow 51,521.22, +0.49%. The move was tech‑led: AMD +4%, AVGO ~+4.3%, NVDA +1.1%, MSFT ~+1.5%. Energy weak on Brent flat‑to‑up; defensives mixed as yield‑proxy names caught the long‑end bid. The reversal reads as the market saying “term premium at 5.70% on 30Y was the pain threshold; back it off and the earnings story carries the day”. For the Fed watchers this is a signal that the near‑end dovish repricing is doing more pricing work than the long‑end stagflation hedge — the implied path says October skip, December hike probability back in the mid‑70s, 2027 terminal rate pricing around 4.50% rather than 4.75%.

What it means for Wednesday’s FOMC minutes. The September meeting delivered the 25bp hike with unanimous backing — but the specific thing the minutes will reveal is how many officials wanted the hike framework extended further and whether the September staff forecast carried an acceleration case for prices or a deceleration case for employment. If dissent language is present on either side — a hawk flagging the need for one more hike in October, or a dove warning that labour softness is already in the pipeline — the Fed path pricing resets intraday. The specific scenario the market is positioning for: the minutes reveal two or three officials noting labour risk, in which case the Williams/Jefferson patience framework becomes consensus and November FOMC becomes an outright skip discussion. Converse: the minutes reveal three or more officials wanting a faster path, in which case Tuesday’s rally reverses hard and the 30Y re‑tests 5.75%.

For the gilt tape and the Budget. The transatlantic correlation is holding at ~70% on the long end: UK 30Y moved with US 30Y Monday to Tuesday, closing ~5.90% Tuesday from Monday’s intraday near 5.95%, down from Friday’s 5.87% close only marginally. The Thursday 6.07% intraday peak is now a visible resistance level the market remembers. The UK‑specific premium (fiscal, political) remains priced inside the global term‑premium story; the 28 October Budget is the specific event that could decouple the two. The Burnham/Healey government’s first Budget faces fiscal headroom somewhere around £10–12 billion (versus £22bn in the spring), and the three‑rate tax commitment (income tax, NI, VAT held) binds. The Chancellor’s choice set has narrowed to savings, property and investment taxes — which has a specific transmission to equity multiples that affects the whole UK‑listed book, and to UKW’s discount via the discount‑rate channel. UK Services PMI final at 52.1 (vs 51.7 flash, prior 52.5) Monday was a slight upside revision but input costs posted the strongest increase since June and prices charged the strongest since May — the UK is pricing through the global fuel inflation at a lag.

03AMD’s OpenAI warrant trade re‑priced into Citi’s $800. Nvidia within one session of $6trn.

The AI chip arms race is now visibly diversifying from Nvidia‑only pricing. AMD closed above $650 Tuesday on sell‑side upgrades referencing MI450 deployment economics; the warrant dilution is now in the mark and the stock trades on pace‑of‑deployment rather than on vesting mechanics.

The structure of the OpenAI deal. AMD committed warrants for up to 160 million shares at a strike of $0.01 to OpenAI, vesting against deployment of 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct MI450 GPUs over multiple years (first 1GW rollout targeted 2026, remaining 5GW through 2029–30), with vesting split into tranches that unlock on reaching sequential deployment milestones and on AMD trading above specific share‑price thresholds — the final tranche carries a $600 threshold that cleared on 21 September and the stock has held above $600 continuously since. A parallel structure sits with Meta (potentially up to a second 160 million share tranche against separate deployment milestones), giving a combined maximum ~320 million shares of future dilution on a ~1.6bn base — call it 17–20% maximum dilution if both warrants fully vest. The Street’s modelling converged over the past week on “dilution is real but it is the price of locking the second‑largest AI compute buyer at non‑trivial scale”.

The Tuesday upgrades. Citi lifted its target to $800 from a prior around $560; Stifel to $700; several other sell‑side desks moved to the $650–$750 range through Monday and Tuesday. The specific analytical case: on 6GW of MI450 at a blended $25k–$30k per GPU equivalent revenue, AMD runs a $45–60bn gross revenue leg from OpenAI alone over four years, which net of warrant dilution still prints a materially better 2027–2029 EPS line than the pre‑deal consensus. The pacing risk: 1GW of GPU deployment requires several hundred megawatts of real‑estate‑linked power capacity that OpenAI, Oracle, Crusoe and the hyperscalers are competing for. AMD’s own loan guarantee for Crusoe ($300m with a chip‑leaseback floor) is the specific signal that it understands the deployment bottleneck sits in physical infrastructure, not silicon capacity.

The move in the stock. AMD closed Monday $631.75 (+3.6%) and Tuesday around $657 (+4%) on the Citi/Stifel upgrades, with the intraday high at $658.52, a fresh 52‑week high. The two‑session move is +7.6% from Friday’s $635.66 close, net +£28 on the 1.62 direct shares. The look‑through through ATT (2.64% weight, ~£125 slice), Royal London, Rathbone, SMH and the semi‑heavy trackers is where the real contribution lands — estimate ~£75–100 on the AMD leg of the sleeve. The specific risk to this trade: the Q3 print early November, when AMD has to reconcile the OpenAI milestones with the Micron HBM4E cost‑ceiling line that showed up in the Micron FQ4 release 24 September. If 2027 gross margin guidance comes in at or below 50%, the dilution plus the margin compression on the premium chip could land as a −10% session even inside the Citi target. The AMD book position is already pricing the upside; the downside risk is thin‑skinned.

Nvidia within one session of $6trn.
