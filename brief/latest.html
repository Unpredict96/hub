02Nvidia: from a governance discount to an accounting question

Last week the market priced the CEO. On Thursday it priced enforcement risk across the chain. Today it priced whether the reported numbers can be trusted. Each step is wider than the one before.

What was reported, and what wasn’t

The facts are thin, so here they are exactly. Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, reported that SEC enforcement staff are looking into potential accounting issues at Nvidia. Reuters could not determine which issues are involved or when the inquiry started. The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond. On Tuesday the WSJ reported that the SEC was examining whether Nvidia’s public disclosures misled investors. Today’s report is the first to put the accounts, not just the disclosures, inside the inquiry. An SEC enforcement inquiry is not a finding. Many close with no action. But the market reads it alongside three facts already on the record:

  • The 10-Q wasn’t filed with results. Nvidia had filed its 10-Q on results day for nine straight quarters. On 17 November it said “in the coming weeks.” One secondary source puts the 40-day filing deadline at about 4 December. A UBS note reported that management linked the timing to “the ongoing investigation and whether any additional risk factors” are needed. Nvidia has not explained it publicly.
  • The board’s independent investigation, opened on 8 November after the reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, is run by independent directors with Joon H. Kim (former acting US Attorney, SDNY) and Wachtell Lipton. Its published scope is narrow. The market is now asking whether it is wider than published.
  • Export-control exposure. The DOJ and Commerce investigation into partner Megaspeed, Monday’s BIS licence rule, the Hunterbrook allegation against AMD on Thursday, and the House Foreign Affairs interviews on 1–2 December.

The board chair, Harvey Jones, has said the board does not intend to reinstate Huang, despite reports that some directors favour it.

03Oil: six cents from the line, then a late rally

The rule passed its first real test. An intraday print came within cents of $90, and the settle came in $2.57 clear. The things that would take Brent below $90 are still there.

The tape

The day ran in three phases. In Asia Brent traded around $92.22. By the European morning it had fallen to $90.33 at 10:32 GMT (Reuters quote via 5paisa) and about $90.06 at the low (Trading Economics CFD, −2.09%). That is the closest Brent has been to the $90.00 line since the three-leg rule was written. In the afternoon it reversed: CNBC had Jan Brent at $92.22 at 2:56pm ET, and the only settle report I found (rallies.ai, Reuters-style copy) gives $92.57, +$0.59 (+0.64%), with WTI at $83.98, +$0.77. The ICE settlement window ends at 19:30 London (14:30 ET), so CNBC’s 14:56 quote is consistent with a settle in the $92–92.6 range. I am treating $92.57 as right to within about 50 cents. That is good enough to say the trigger did not fire, because missing it would take a $2.57 error.

Week: Brent from $95.84 (13 November settle) to $92.57 is about −3.4%, the third weekly decline in a row. Reports of 4–6% were all intraday snapshots. WTI from $88.56 to $83.98 is −5.2%.

Why it fell, and why it came back

The bearish case is simple: the barrels are moving. Oilprice.com/Yahoo argues that Gulf export flows to Asian buyers have recovered to near pre-war levels, and that this outweighs one confirmed tanker hit per day in Hormuz and the Houthi threats. With the IEA calling a return to surplus, OPEC+ meeting on 29 November to set 2027 policy, and US drilling picking up, there is a lot pushing the price down. The risk-off move added to it: Saxo pointed to the AI unwind and wider risk aversion as weighing on crude, which is the first time this month that an equity sell-off has fed into oil.

The rally that rescued it was short-covering, not news. Funds that had sold through three weekly declines bought back ahead of a holiday-thinned US week (Thanksgiving is on 26 November). Iranian risk got the credit in the copy, but nothing new happened on the Iranian side between 10:30 GMT and the settle. So the $92.57 doesn’t mean the downtrend has turned. Positioning was simply too short going into a weekend.

04Rates and the UK: bonds rallied, and retail sales beat

Treasuries did their job today. The Fed’s December decision didn’t move, and the UK data was stronger than the market can comfortably take at face value.

US. The 10-year fell below 5% in the European session (FXStreet) and was about 4.95–4.97% into the close (CNBC 4.974%, Investing.com 4.948% at 11:37 ET), down roughly 10bp. The 2-year was at about 4.70%. This was a move into safety, not a repricing of the Fed: December is still fully priced for 25bp (FXStreet), and Governor Waller’s call for 50bp is still the hawkish tail. The bond market followed risk assets today, not Fed rhetoric. For your book, a lower 10-year is a small help to UKW and GSK through the UK duration channel, but it didn’t help UKW today (s6).

UK. October retail sales volumes rose 0.7% m/m against a consensus of about −0.1%. September was revised down to −0.6% from −0.4%, so the two-month level is less impressive than the headline. The year-on-year figure is about +2.3% (sources differ between 1.7% and 2.3%). Food stores drove it, with supermarkets reporting higher sales of tinned goods and UHT milk after the government launched its “Be Ready” resilience campaign on 22 October. Non-food was up 1.1%. Public sector borrowing for October was about £17bn, with debt interest up sharply (MLQ.ai), which is a reminder of why gilts have been carrying a fiscal premium. I have no verified gilt close for today. The last verified levels are the 10-year above 5.3% and the 2-year above 5% on 16 November.

What the beat means for the BoE: the MPC has been about 80% priced for a December hike since CPI. A retail beat driven by precautionary stockpiling is weak evidence of underlying demand, and the MPC will probably discount it. The more relevant point is that if households are stockpiling because the government told them to prepare, that is an inflation impulse on food, which is the kind the BoE has been most worried about. On balance this nudges toward a hike rather than away from one. The 17 December MPC is the event for UKW and the UK parts of the fund sleeve.

FX. GBP/USD closed at 1.3086 (−0.02%), in a tight 1.3085–1.3096 range. The dollar didn’t benefit from risk-off, which fits with Treasury yields falling. The pound’s flat day meant USD lines came through to sterling almost unchanged. GBP/EUR was about 1.2006 on Wednesday’s Prestige print, with nothing newer.
