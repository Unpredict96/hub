02Shell Q3 trading update: $42/bbl refining margin, LNG held, Integrated Gas lift — and a €2.5bn cash drag

Pre‑market before the LSE open. The headline is the refining margin. The composition is the real story: three defensive legs now visibly working at the same time, offset by a Q3 cash timing drag that pushes the full read to the 29 October print.

The headline numbers

Shell’s Q3 2026 interim trading update, released pre‑market Wednesday ahead of the full Q3 results scheduled for Thursday 29 October, carried four lines that matter for the equity:

  • Indicative Q3 refining margin $42/bbl against Q2’s $24/bbl. A +75% quarter‑on‑quarter jump, the largest since the 2022 window, with refinery utilisation guided to 93–97% — top of the historical range. The driver mix: Middle East supply disruption (Qatar FM, Iran war fuel crisis), ongoing Russian refining attacks limiting diesel supply into Europe, and the specific tightness in distillate that the G7 100mb release is now beginning to physically address. The $42 print is also almost certainly above sell‑side consensus, which had been running around $28–32.
  • Q3 LNG liquefaction volumes 7.2–7.6 million tonnes vs Q2’s 7.7mt — a very mild quarterly softening, consistent with Qatar FM pulling the global market tighter rather than looser on supply, which lifts price and favours the integrated producer. LNG trading and optimisation guided to mirror Q2 — so no step down on the trading contribution, which was the specific line sell side had been de‑risking into the update.
  • Q3 Integrated Gas production 740–780 kboe/d vs Q2’s 631 kboe/d — a ~17% uplift primarily reflecting ARC Resources acquisition completed on 2 September landing in the P&L for roughly one month of the quarter. The Q4 run‑rate uplift will be considerably larger as the full quarter’s ARC contribution lands. ARC closed at a $7bn equity value / Shell’s 40% stake nets ~16 kboe/d of Montney gas with a long reserves tail. The Q4 implied Integrated Gas run‑rate on this update is roughly 800–820 kboe/d, materially above the pre‑ARC trajectory.
  • Q3 upstream production and midstream both guided broadly in line with Q2, no material surprise in either direction. Refinery utilisation at the top end of the range confirms the margin print is volume‑supported, not a thin‑throughput read.

The counterweights

The sell side pulled two lines out of the update immediately as the mitigating offset:

  • €2.5bn German emissions‑certificate cash outflow in Q3. Historically this has fallen in Q4; this quarter it has been pulled forward into Q3. The read: this is a timing shift, not a new charge — the FY cash flow is unchanged, but the Q3 group operating cash flow line will show the drag the market would have otherwise seen at Q4. For modelling purposes this leaks around €2bn of free cash flow from the quarter’s net print. The pointed question for the 29 October release: whether buyback cadence is maintained at the current €4.2bn/quarter level into Q4 despite the timing drag.
  • Higher net debt from acquisition‑related costs plus the emissions timing. Shell’s net debt had been running around $38bn end‑Q2; the ARC closing cash outflow (~$3.1bn in cash plus share consideration) plus the emissions timing could push end‑Q3 net debt toward $42–44bn. The gearing ratio stays comfortably within the 15–20% range at this level, but the specific implication is that the Q3 dividend announcement on 29 October will not be the vehicle for a step‑up: expect the maintained 36.0c payout rather than a surprise lift.
  • Low Rhine water levels affecting Rheinland refinery. A continuation of the Q2 problem; not new. The specific tell is that despite this, group refinery utilisation still prints 93–97%, which means other European and US sites have been running flat out.

What it means for your position

Shell at +£4,344 unrealised, +160% on cost coming into the week; after the +£58 Wednesday move the position sits at roughly +£4,400. The specific framing: the three defensive legs (LNG decoupling, refining through the diesel squeeze, upstream through Brent’s $100 floor) are now visibly working simultaneously, and the Q3 update was arguably the first clean quarter where all three legs printed positive in the same window. The LNG decoupling thesis the stock has traded on for weeks has been reinforced by the Phase 2 FID (29 September, now in the mark). The refining leg has a hard number ($42/bbl, +75% QoQ). The upstream leg benefits from Brent $100 holding on OPEC+ steady and Hormuz tension. The question that remains for the trim conversation (idea B from the 3 October weekly review): what breaks any of these three legs. The specific scenarios: (i) TTF retracing through €60 on Qatar FM resolution or warmer winter — the LNG leg weakens; (ii) G7 release landing physically in the diesel market in sufficient volume to drive the crack down — the refining leg compresses; (iii) OPEC+ reversing in December and adding 500kb/d — the upstream floor weakens. None of these are the base case for Q4 but any of them would open the trim conversation. The CGT gate on £4,400 of unrealised gain still binds in the General account.

Reaction in the stock was muted relative to the operational print: LSE close 3,661p, +0.8% on Tuesday’s 3,631.5p. The specific explanation is the €2.5bn cash outflow line and the net debt lift, which the market reads as a signal that the Q3 formal results on 29 October may carry a tempered buyback cadence or dividend tone despite the margin beat. BP by contrast, with no LNG offset and no Montney‑scale acquisition, closed broadly flat on Tuesday’s 561.7p per the LSE board. The Shell‑over‑BP spread continues to widen on fundamentals rather than on multiple.

The one line to carry to 29 OctoberThe specific Q3 release read is now: did the full print retain the $42/bbl refining margin number, did LNG trading hold its 25% of Integrated Gas earnings share, and did management maintain the €4.2bn/quarter buyback cadence despite the emissions‑timing cash drag. Positive on all three = the Shell trim conversation stays off the table through year end. Negative on any one = the trim question re‑opens for the first time since the Phase 2 FID landed.

03FOMC minutes read hawkish — one more hike by year‑end — and the long end widened on both sides of the Atlantic

The September minutes landed at 14:00 ET / 19:00 BST and reset the Fed path pricing hawkish, undoing most of the dovish repricing Friday’s NFP had delivered. The long end took it harder than the near end.

The minutes specifically. The release carried three lines the Fed watchers were looking for and all three leaned hawkish. First, most participants assessed that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate by year end — not “some”, not “a number of”, but “most”, which in FOMC minutes convention means a working majority of the 18 participants. Second, the median policymaker’s own projection put the federal funds rate at 4.1% by year end, which from the current 3.75–4.00% range implies one more 25bp hike in either October or December, consistent with the Chair Warsh framework from the Jackson Hole address. Third, the 12‑0 vote on the September 25bp hike was unanimous — no dissent language on either side, so no dovish counterweight to flag. The combined read: the September meeting was not the terminal move, the near‑end dovish repricing on Friday’s NFP was overdone, and the market needs to re‑price the probability of November FOMC action higher.

The near‑term re‑pricing. Pre‑minutes, October probability of a hike was running ~17–20% and December ~75–82%. Post‑minutes, the probability of at least one more hike by year end moved from the mid‑70s to around 87% — a 10–15pp hawkish shift in a single session. The 2027 terminal rate pricing re‑anchored around 4.50–4.75% from Tuesday’s 4.50% consensus.
