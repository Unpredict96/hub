01The five-minute version

A single PMI print did the work of a hawkish speech, an oil rally and a fiscal warning combined.

One. The US flash composite PMI printed 58.4 — the strongest private-sector expansion since July 2021 — and the bond market broke.

The composite came in at 58.4 from 56.0 in August, services 58.7 from 54.6, manufacturing 57.0 from 53.9. The New Orders Index rose to 58.2, the highest since March 2022. But the piece the market priced was the Business Input Prices Index at 66.4, the highest since October 2022 — growth and inflation together, the specific combination the Warsh Fed has said would justify another hike. Add a poor five-year Treasury auction and the belly of the curve gave way: 10Y +14bp to 5.116%, a 19-year high (July 2007); 5Y through 5% for the first time since 2007. The move was the largest single-session 10Y sell-off in nearly 18 months. Every bit of Monday’s AI melt-up and Tuesday’s record close was subject to that repricing. Nasdaq −1.11% to 26,936, Russell 2000 −1.60%, S&P 500 essentially flat at ~7,757, Dow −0.30% to ~51,707. The small-cap tell is the clean read: this was a duration event, not a growth-scare.

Two. Sterling took the double hit — strong US, soft UK services — and cleared 1.33 to the downside.

UK flash composite 51.7 from 52.5, services 51.7 vs 52.0 expected, only manufacturing beat (52.0 vs 51.5). Chris Williamson called it a “worrying combination of disappointingly sluggish economic growth and intensifying inflationary pressures.” The eurozone print was the opposite: composite 53.1, a 10-month high, services 53.0 from 51.6, manufacturing steady at 52.7. So sterling was squeezed on both sides — USD bid on the US boom, EUR bid on the eurozone beat, GBP left to price the domestic disappointment. GBP/USD 1.3253 by London close, the weakest since 1 July, down ~0.78% on the day and now down ~1.75% over the four sessions since 18 September. That is worth ~£720 in reported value to you on the dollar look-through today alone and it is close to the entire day’s book gain.

Three. Shell gave back more in one session than four crude down-days took, even as Brent rebounded intraday.

Brent traded sub-$99 in the morning on the continued Iran/Qatar thread, then rebounded to $101.61, +2.37% on the Bessent airline sanction landing and Iranian parliament noise on nuclear-treaty withdrawal. Shell did not participate. SHEL closed 3,375.5p, −95.5p, −2.72% on the day, and BP was worse at −14.1p to 565.1p. The tape sold LNG-integrated names because TTF cleared €73/MWh to the downside, a three-week low, and the front-month gas fade is now what’s driving Shell rather than the crude tick. Your position value is ~£6,514 tonight vs £6,774 yesterday, −£260. The trim thesis that never got off the ground on the tanker-premium unwind is now a live conversation, but nothing to execute inside a CGT-loaded General account tonight (see section 06).

02The 10-year broke through 5%

One PMI print, a poor five-year auction and an oil rebound. Enough.

The mechanism was linear and it did not need embroidery. S&P Global US flash composite PMI: 58.4 in September, up from 56.0 in August, the highest print since July 2021. Consensus wanted something modestly below trend. What it got was services 58.7 from 54.6, manufacturing 57.0 from 53.9, new orders at 58.2 (highest since March 2022), and input prices at 66.4, the highest since October 2022. Chris Williamson’s summary was that historical comparisons put annualised growth at around 5%. The point that matters for your book is not the growth print but the input-prices sub-index — growth and inflation printing together at these levels is exactly the combination Warsh described at Jackson Hole as the standard for further tightening. If the market had a way to price “probability of a second hike by year-end”, it added meaningfully to it today.

The bond market did the pricing directly. 10Y Treasury +14bp to 5.116%, a 19-year high (last seen July 2007). The move gained steam after the 10Y broke through the 5% level cleanly — there was no technical hesitation around a psychologically important number, which is itself an information: the fast money was not there to defend it. 5Y through 5% for the first time since 2007, and a weak five-year Treasury auction met poor demand, which added a tail to the belly move. 2Y +~6bp to ~4.78%. This was the biggest one-day 10Y sell-off in nearly 18 months.

The equity read was clean. The Nasdaq −1.11% and Russell 2000 −1.60% together are the small-cap-heavy tell: rate-sensitive names took the pain, not the AI-agnostic mega-caps. AMD gave back only mildly (from $620.08 close to ~$613 intraday reference; day range $608.23–$624.69, so not a break of Monday’s Muse-CPU support). Nvidia essentially flat at ~$228.87. Meta held $749.69. The AI-CPU trade did not break; it took its first breath in five sessions. The Dow lost 157 pts on defensive banks-and-cyclicals action and the S&P finished basically flat, ~7,757. Nothing here is a growth scare — it is a repricing of what the terminal rate has to be to bring services PMI at 58.7 back to trend.

The dollar was the collateral. DXY firmed against everything except the euro. GBP/USD did the extra pain because it took both sides of the print — strong US surprise, soft UK services. Yen weakened. Euro largely held on the German PMI beat. The cumulative sterling loss since 18 September is now approximately −1.75% or £1,600 into the dollar look-through — a real number, and one that is beginning to be worth naming as a running position rather than daily noise.

What the desk should watch tomorrowThe 10Y through 5% has three second-order effects that matter for the book. First, if it holds above 5% into US Q2 GDP third estimate tomorrow (consensus 3.3%) and Friday’s core PCE, mortgage rates cross the level that begins to break housing forward indicators, which is what would eventually give the equity tape something more than a giveback to price. Second, the long-end global sell-off catches UK gilts: the 30Y that rallied 12bp on the QT rewrite two weeks ago has to give some of that back, and the UKW discount-rate lever moves gently against you until the November MPC or the Autumn Budget resets it. Third, the FX effect that has flattered your dollar sleeve for a fortnight is not free money — the same mechanism reverses on a soft NFP on 3 October or a soft CPI on 15 October, and the cumulative sterling weakness now looks stretched enough that mean-reversion is a live tail. Not tonight’s worry, but a specific tail to size against.

03The AI-CPU trade took its first breath

Not a break. A giveback that respected Monday’s support.

Look at the intraday map before deciding what to make of the tech tape. AMD traded a day range of $608.23 to $624.69, with the intraday reference at $613.87. Tuesday’s close was $620.08. Monday’s Muse-CPU spike closed at $609.38 — and the low of today’s range did not violate Monday’s close. That is the specific piece of information that matters. If the Muse rally were a squeeze, today’s yield-driven risk-off would have been the day it broke; the fact that support held on a day when 10Y yields moved 14bp is the third-session confirmation the CPU repricing was real. Broadcom did give back the most (~−2.5% to $353.59), but that is the highest-multiple, longest-duration name in the chip complex and it was the natural place to trim into a rate move. Nvidia effectively flat at $228.87 — a full day range of just $3.48, which is a very quiet Nvidia tape and reads as neither buyers nor sellers convinced. Meta closed $749.69, down 0.3% from Tuesday’s $752 est — the Wells Fargo $796 target held, no follow-through, no break.

Allianz Technology Trust touched a fresh 52-week high of 765p intraday. The NAV re-rate that has been pending in the tape since Monday’s Muse rally landed as expected: by 09:06 London time ATT was at 761.98p, up ~1.1%, and above the 764p prior 52-week peak. The late-session US tech giveback probably capped the London close below the intraday high; working range 758–762p, and I could not verify the actual settle tonight. That is a ~5.5–5.9% gain on the 619 shares from Friday’s 720p mark, worth ~£250 into tomorrow’s II mark. The direct-holding footprint on all of this is small enough that the P&L barely moves — 1.6175 AMD shares add ~£3 to the day at the current FX; the ~0.7 AVGO adds nothing; the META fraction rounds to zero. The material AI exposure sits inside the funds and the fund sleeve absorbed today’s Nasdaq −1.11% into overnight NAVs that publish through the morning. The trade you own vs the trade you would sizeYou continue to own the AI trade in the right size through funds. The direct positions are token exposures that add nothing to conviction. The question that would have come up tonight in the aftermath of Monday and Tuesday — do you add back the 60 ATT shares? — is not more compelling at 760p+ than it was at 690p when the trim went off. At 690p the trim was against a Nvidia-concentration concern that hasn’t been retested. At 760p+ the same Nvidia concentration exists, sitting on a name (NVDA at $228.87) that had a $3.48 range today. The trim thesis is not urgent to reverse.
