02Nvidia: the rule is closed, the stock isn’t, and the line is £275

The result answered the fundamental question. The fade shows the market is now pricing governance. The share counts show that, for this book, neither matters as much as the last fortnight of briefs implied.

The last condition

Yesterday’s brief set the FY28 test before the call so that the after-hours move could not decide it. It would fire if Kress withdrew the roughly 70% framing, cut it below about 50%, or stopped giving multi-year framing. Instead, management pointed to about 75% FY28 revenue growth (Yahoo Finance; Motley Fool). Kress called the demand outlook “explosive”. The higher base matters here, because 75% on a year that has already outrun August’s guide is a bigger absolute number than 70% was then. BofA called the call “very reassuring” and kept its $280 target.

Two items from yesterday’s list of things to listen for are still unanswered. Why a beat-and-raise closed lower

The stock’s range was $150.40–159.70, so it gave up about $8.70 from the high, and volume was about 117% above average. Three causes, in order of how much evidence I have for each. (1) Succession. The chair told the call there are no plans to reinstate Huang, and the company ruled out questions about him. None of the eight analysts raised him, and neither Kress nor Jones mentioned him. Yahoo graded Kress A on the numbers and C on optics, and Jones C−. KeyBanc’s Vinh says the open succession keeps the stock range-bound. (2) China and Washington. Huang and Kress both face congressional interviews (Huang on 1 December, the general counsel on 2 December), and Monday’s BIS rule is still being absorbed. (3) The quality of the beat. As set out yesterday, the 14-week quarter means the Q4 guide is about +4% sequential like-for-like, and the margin step-down is real even though it was flagged.

The peer moves are the useful signal. AMD and Broadcom rose, TSM gained 0.81% and ASML 0.88%. If the market had doubted AI demand, the suppliers would have fallen with Nvidia. They didn’t. The discount is specific to Nvidia, and capex demand is not being questioned. That was Monday’s argument for why a clean print helps TSM and ASML more than NVDA, and one session is consistent with it. One session does not prove it.

04Rates: UK CPI a tenth light, the Fed minutes hawkish

The BoE got some room; the Fed made it clear it doesn’t think it has any. In this book the gap between them shows up mainly through sterling.

UK

October CPI was 3.6% y/y, up from 3.5%. Market consensus was 3.7% (FXStreet), and Pound Sterling Live gives the BoE’s own forecast as 3.73%. Yesterday’s brief carried a 3.6% consensus. Sources disagree, but the market traded it as a miss. Core 2.8%, unchanged and in line. Services 3.7% against the BoE’s 3.9%, which is the most important number in the release. December hike pricing fell from about 90% to about 80%. Gilts rose 78 ticks and outperformed Bunds (+4). I can’t verify a closing 2-year or 10-year yield. The last levels I have are the 2-year just above 5% and the 10-year above 5.35% on 16 November. A 78-tick rally in the futures roughly implies 8–10bp lower at the 10-year, but that is an inference, not a print. The BoE’s unscheduled reminder that it expects inflation to rise in 2027 looks like an attempt to stop the market treating one soft services print as a turn. One economist still expects CPI above 4% early next year.

For the book: UKW rose 0.5% on the gilt rally, the right direction for a duration-sensitive trust that pays its dividend on 27 November. The WCfD consultation closes on 9 December. GSK fell 0.59% and has not recovered from Tuesday’s unexplained 3.14% fall. GAW rose 2.05% and gave back none of it.

US

The minutes of the 27–28 October meeting, at which the Fed held at 3.75–4.00% with Cook and Hammack dissenting for +25bp, show “almost all” participants expecting further hikes. A couple said a move larger than 25bp could be warranted in December if the inflation data disappointed. Almost all discussed the risk that returns on AI investment disappoint. That reads across to the Nvidia lending story and to the fund sleeve’s large US tech weight. Waller argued for 50bp, or a clearer signal that more tightening is coming, on the grounds that about 70% of core services components are running above 3%. CNBC has a 25bp December hike about 95% priced. The question now is whether the move is 25bp or 50bp, not whether there is one.

The curve flattened. The 2-year rose 1.5bp to 4.804% and the 10-year fell 3.5bp to 5.068%. Housing starts came in at 1.309m against 1.245m expected, and pending home sales fell 4.7% against −2.0% expected. Gold fell 3.17% to about $3,727, silver 4.78%, and bitcoin 3.72%. A firm dollar and higher real-rate expectations hit hard assets.

FX

GBP/USD closed at 1.3144, its lowest since 14 October. It fell from 1.3193 to 1.3172 within ten minutes of the CPI release, and kept falling as the Fed minutes and Waller took the dollar higher. EUR/USD was 1.0973 and EUR/GBP traded 0.8320–0.8336, so GBP/EUR is about 1.198–1.20. On roughly £80k of US look-through plus the £11.8k of direct US lines, a 0.5% fall in sterling adds about £450 in GBP terms. That is why the fund sleeve should be roughly flat tomorrow even though the S&P fell, and why BRK-B lost £150 rather than £160.
