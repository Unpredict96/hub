01The five-minute version

One. NFP at 29k. October is off the table, December is wobbling.

The September Employment Situation Report came in at +29,000 payrolls against a Dow Jones consensus of +89,000, and the Reuters survey of +100k that both yesterday’s brief and the ADP +90k setup had implied as the base case. The unemployment rate ticked up from 4.1% to 4.2%, which was itself a tick firmer than consensus 4.2% carried — it looked exactly as weak as the headline. Average hourly earnings grew 0.1% month‑on‑month to $37.81, taking the annual pace to 3.0% year‑on‑year — the softest wage growth since early 2024. Previous months were revised lower, which is the single detail that nukes the “one weak print” dismissal: the trend is bending, not spiking. CME FedWatch odds for an October hike collapsed from Thursday’s 42% to around 17% through the US session, with some sell‑side taking it as low as 15% once the hourly earnings line printed. December is still pricing a hike, but the odds have slipped from just under 90% to the mid‑70s. Two. The G7 agreed a 100‑million‑barrel reserves release. Oil broke $100 and faded.

At an emergency IEA session chaired by French President Emmanuel Macron, the G7 agreed to a coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels of emergency oil stocks over the next four months, “weighted toward diesel and crude”, with a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners. The move follows two days of US pressure — the US said it would release another 40 million barrels from its own SPR and threatened a diesel export ban if Europe did not match — and an initial EU statement from Energy Commissioner Jørgensen on Thursday that a reserves release “is a possibility”. This is the first coordinated IEA release since March 2026’s 400‑million‑barrel action, and it specifically addresses the European diesel squeeze that followed China’s fuel export suspension on Thursday. Brent broke $100 intraday on the pre‑release speculation, then faded to $99.68 at the close, −2.57% on the day. The specific thing to watch is whether 100mb is enough: on a frontloaded basis that is roughly 1.2mb/d of extra supply through the first three weeks, against a European diesel deficit the market has been pricing at 150–200kb/d. Enough to cap the panic; not obviously enough to push cracks structurally lower. The 100‑barrel line is a tactical, not structural, intervention. Oil majors held up through the session — Shell was +0.15% to 3,594.50p, BP was +0.13% to 557.80p — because the refining margin trade carries both ways: a diesel release caps crude but leaves refining spreads vulnerable to normalisation inside 30 days.

02NFP at 29k is the number that mattered — October is off the table

The weakest payrolls print of this cycle, with prior revisions lower and unemployment up. The curve round‑tripped but hike odds collapsed. Stocks took it as unambiguously dovish.

The release. The BLS Employment Situation Report for September landed at 13:30 BST with payrolls at +29,000 against a Dow Jones consensus of +89,000 — a 60k miss on the single data point the Fed watches most closely. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2% from 4.1%, matching the softer edge of consensus. Average hourly earnings grew 0.1% month‑on‑month to $37.81, taking the annual pace to 3.0% year‑on‑year — the softest wage print since early 2024, and the specific line the bulls had hoped to anchor against. Prior months were revised lower in line with the summer revision cycle. Labour force participation held. Household survey had unemployment claims consistent with the weekly 200k read Thursday showed. None of the sub‑components spin: this is a weak print at every line.

The curve reaction. Immediate: 10‑year rallied to 5.15% in the first 20 minutes, a 10bp move lower that matched the headline shock. Then it round‑tripped. By the US close the 10‑year sat at 5.28%, +5bp on Thursday’s 5.24%, the opposite direction of the ISM‑driven move Thursday. The round‑trip is the specific thing to read — the first-order reaction was “Fed holds in October”, but the second‑order reaction was “the stagflation combination of ISM 54.5 and NFP 29k keeps December in play and keeps the 2027 terminal rate wider”. The 2‑year rallied harder and held: closed around 4.90%, −17bp, as fast money took the dovish re‑pricing on the near end but hedged the long end against the inflation story. The curve bull‑steepened materially — the 2s10s spread blew out around 20bp wider in a session.

The hike‑odds reaction was decisive on the near end. CME FedWatch for October collapsed from Thursday’s ~42% to ~17% at the US close, with some sell‑side taking it as low as 15% intraday. December fell from just under 90% to the mid‑70s — still priced for a hike, but no longer a near‑certainty. The 15% probability on a second hike by Q1 2027 that Thursday carried has slipped to under 10%. The reaction is not quite complete: Williams speaks again next week, and the FOMC September minutes land Wednesday 8 October — those two inputs set whether the market treats today as a trend inflection or a one‑print aberration.

What it means for the gilt tape and the Budget. The dovish US print was the single most important input for UK long rates all week — the global bond rout that broke the 30‑year gilt through 6% on Thursday was synchronous with the US 10‑year running to 5.34%. The reverse is now operating: the gilt bull‑steepening landed through the US morning, with UK 10Y closing 5.34% (likely about 10bp tighter than Thursday) and UK 30Y at 5.87% after an intraday retrace through 6.00%. The Chancellor’s 28 October Budget frame is marginally easier tonight than it was yesterday evening, though the structural concern around a £30bn tax package is unchanged. The specific thing to monitor through next week is the gilt vol surface: a drift back to 5.75% on the 30‑year by Wednesday’s FOMC minutes is the signal the fiscal‑capacity concern is receding; a push back through 6% is the signal that the UK‑specific premium is becoming the dominant pricing input independent of global rates.

The US equity reaction read clean. S&P 500 +0.49% to 7,722.93, Nasdaq +1.19% to 27,190.86, Dow +0.49% to 51,177.44. The Nasdaq-led tape makes sense — semi complex was long duration via valuation sensitivity to the hike path, and the dovish repricing is a direct multiple expansion input. The specific line to carry: this is a bull steepener plus Nasdaq rally session — the pattern that favours long‑duration growth equity, housing, and REITs, and penalises banks and energy. The combination hasn’t shown up cleanly all cycle because the macro has been bull‑flattening on growth concern or bear‑steepening on inflation concern; today was the first bull‑steepener on genuine labour deterioration.

What to watch into next weekThree inputs set whether today is a one‑print aberration or a trend inflection. (i) Williams and other FOMC speakers Monday–Tuesday — if the dovish camp lets this print stand without pushback, the October skip is locked. (ii) FOMC September minutes Wednesday 8 October — the specific line to read is dissent language: a second dissenter against the Jackson Hole hike framework is the setup for a Powell pivot through November. (iii) US September CPI Wednesday 15 October — the single data point that resets December pricing. A core print at 3.0% YoY or below opens an outright skip; above 3.1% keeps December in play. The ADP October print on Wednesday 5 November will carry more weight than normal as the next labour read.

03The G7 reserves release broke oil’s $100 ceiling. Gas kept going the other way.

100 million barrels over four months with a frontloaded diesel release. Brent faded from above $100 to $99.68. TTF added another 3% because the gas story is physical, not refined product.

What happened at the IEA. French President Emmanuel Macron chaired an emergency IEA session Friday with the full G7 agreeing to a coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels of emergency oil stocks over the next four months, “weighted toward diesel and crude”. The specific commitment language is unusually precise: a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners. The move came after two days of escalating US pressure: Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Wednesday several European countries had delivered only a fraction of their earlier release pledges, the US released a further 40 million barrels from its own SPR on Thursday, and the Thursday evening message was that the US would consider a diesel export ban if Europe did not match. Germany reportedly retained around 15 million barrels of its earlier pledge, representing 77% of its overall commitment — today’s coordinated action compels delivery of what remained outstanding.

The scale calibration. 100mb over four months is roughly 820kb/d on an averaged basis. Weighted toward the first 20 days as the release language specifies, that is closer to 1.2mb/d of extra supply through October, then dropping to 700kb/d through November–January. The European diesel deficit the market was pricing after Thursday’s China fuel export suspension sat around 150–200kb/d; the release more than covers that at the near end, which is why the Brent reaction was clean and specifically oriented toward crude cracks. The specific thing worth noting is that this is the first IEA coordinated release since the March 2026 400‑million‑barrel action — policy frequency is running at twice the normal pace, which is the Street’s cue that the current oil cycle is being managed inside the political fiscal calendar rather than left to the market.

The price reaction. Brent broke $100 intraday to around $100.50 on the pre‑announcement speculation, then faded through the US morning to settle at $99.68, −2.57% on Thursday. The move is modest — a bigger release would have taken Brent to $95 — which is the specific confirmation that the market reads 100mb as tactical rather than structural. The curve says the same: Brent Nov‑26 fell more than Dec‑26, flattening the near-end contango and signalling that physical traders expect the release to normalise the frontspread without resetting the overall curve.

The read for your Shell position. SHEL closed 3,594.50p +0.15% on a session the headline oil move would have implied −1 to −2%. That is the LNG buffer plus refining margin uplift doing its work: TTF ran +3% to €76.16 (see Section 5), and the European diesel release normalises the refining spread window the sell‑side had pencilled into Q4 but doesn’t immediately crack it. BP held similarly — 557.80p +0.13% — because despite having no LNG offset, the near‑term diesel release doesn’t touch its upstream economics. The specific risk the next five sessions is the sell‑side revision cycle: if Street Q4 margin estimates for Shell / BP get cut by 2–3% on the diesel release, the equity re-rates through to the Q3 print on 29 October. The thesis that Shell is the LNG‑buffered oil major holds; the vulnerability is that the sell‑side is slow to differentiate between integrated LNG majors and pure upstream names on a refining margin shock.

The read for the inflation complex. This is the dovish supply response the Fed and the ECB both needed. Brent at $99 instead of $103 cuts roughly 10bp off the forward headline inflation projection in the eurozone and lifts the ECB cut window for December. In the UK the pass‑through is slower, but the direction is unambiguously disinflationary at the retail fuel line. The RPI print for September on 15 October now has a specific downside skew it did not have 48 hours ago. In the US the direct inflation effect of the SPR release is near zero (the US doesn’t set its own gasoline price off Brent), but the dollar read‑through is marginal weakness — which is why GBP/USD held at 1.3236 despite the dovish US rate shift.

04UKW did the thing. The question is whether it holds.

110.20p close vs Thursday’s estimated 107–108p. +£20 recovered. The 15 October Capital Markets Event is the next catalyst and the specific test of whether the discount compresses structurally.

The move. UKW.L closed 110.20p on Friday per Stockopedia / HL / LSE close, recovering roughly 2.5p from Thursday’s estimated 107–108p close as the 30‑year gilt retraced from 6.07% intraday Thursday to 5.87%, −7bp on the day. On the 8,349 shares that is +£20 recovered, give or take a point either way on the entry mark. The position sits at approximately £9,200 against a £10,075 book cost — still carrying an unrealised loss but inside the trading band the thesis has been working. The specific thing the move confirms is the discount‑rate transmission mechanism: on a day when the 30‑year moved 20bp in UKW’s favour, the implied discount rate used in the trust’s NAV compressed by roughly 15bp, which on a 10‑year weighted asset duration translates to about a 2.5% price move on the equity. That is exactly what we got.

What this means for the 15 October decision frame. The Capital Markets Event at The Ned on 15 October at 13:30 remains the specific line in the sand. Management now has roughly two weeks of breathing room through the gilt move: it can present a buyback escalation / dividend reset / asset recycling package against a backdrop of the long end at 5.87% rather than 6.07%, which is the difference between “defending the discount from a position of weakness” and “confirming the discount from a position of recovering strength”. The specific announcements the market is pricing: (i) a buyback uplift from the current £50m annual envelope to £75–100m; (ii) an explicit dividend policy restatement for 2027 at 7.65p/share minimum, with a stated inflation link; (iii) a disposal of 1–2 operating assets totalling 50–100MW, confirmed at a premium to NAV. A clean sweep on all three could compress the discount by 300–400bp inside a session and take the equity to 118–122p. A disappointment on all three leaves the equity vulnerable to a retest of the 105p area if gilts move wider again.

The 28 October Budget sits as the second catalyst. A benign tax package without an energy windfall measure plus a DMO issuance shift to the short end would rally the 30‑year gilt by 15–25bp on announcement and take UKW toward 115–118p. A windfall energy measure specifically targeting operating renewables (an extension of the Electricity Generator Levy at a punitive rate, or a cap on CfD contract renegotiation flexibility) would take UKW to 100p regardless of the gilt move — this is the tail risk Section 2 of Thursday’s brief flagged. The specific signal to watch between now and the Budget is Treasury committee readouts and any sector consultation line from BEIS; the Chancellor’s choice is between a windfall tax that raises c.£2bn and a corporation tax rate adjustment that raises c.£5bn — the political arithmetic favours corporation tax, which is UKW‑neutral.

Position management from here. The position does the work of being short duration directly inside the ISA. Sizing (£9,200 against a £174,000 book) is roughly 5.3% of the portfolio — small enough that the binary outcome of 15 October + 28 October is a holdable sit. The specific thing not to do between now and the CME: add to the position. The thesis is working inside the decision frame; adding before the CME would turn a 5.3% weight into an 8% weight on a binary event, which is bigger risk than this book sizing supports. If the CME goes well and the equity runs toward 115p, the question becomes whether to trim the position back toward 100p of book cost; if the CME disappoints and the equity breaks to 100p, the thesis is wrong and the position cuts, not averages down. Either way, the trade is static through 15 October.
