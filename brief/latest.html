The $1.10 step is approximately $450m per quarter at the current Integrated Gas production footprint — broadly consistent with Q2’s $400–500m per $1 of realised price sensitivity.

The step confirms that management is treating the leg‑1 and leg‑2 price environment as durable enough to accelerate the capital‑returns programme rather than preserve balance‑sheet flexibility against an unknown winter.

At the current share price of 3,956.5p, $5bn across roughly three months implies approximately 1.3% of market cap repurchased per quarter at current levels, or an approximately 5.2% annualised run‑rate if the pace held. In the twelve months to Q3, Shell has returned 10% plus of the equity through buybacks and dividends combined — the specific capital‑returns intensity that supports the JPMorgan GBp 4,100 target.

Shell also approved LNG Canada Phase 2 at the Q3 release. The project will approximately double capacity at the Kitimat facility from 14mtpa to 28mtpa, with first LNG from the Phase 2 trains expected in the early 2030s. The specific framing: this is the single largest LNG capacity addition Shell has committed to since LNG Canada Phase 1 was greenlit in 2018. It materially extends the Integrated Gas growth runway beyond the 2026 ARC Resources step. For the thesis, Phase 2 confirms that Shell management is willing to deploy capital into long‑dated LNG at the current gas price strip, consistent with the view that structural tightness in Pacific‑basin LNG persists into the 2030s.
