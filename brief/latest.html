03The Fed held 10–2 — what Warsh said and what he didn’t

The 10–2 split with both dissents coming from the hawkish side is the specific detail to carry. Warsh used the press conference to call 2% a hard target and to describe the pause as watching the September hike transmit. He did not signal December.

The decision

FOMC voted 10–2 to maintain the target range at 3.75–4.00%. The dissents: Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, both in favour of an immediate 25bp hike. Two hawkish dissents on an FOMC held is unusual — it reinforces the message that the committee is already debating when, not whether, to hike again. The specific statement language changes that matter:

  • Activity description upgraded from “solid” to “strong”. The specific signal that Tuesday’s consumer confidence beat (+7.5pts to 91.7) and the September hot CPI/PPI data have been absorbed by the committee as data points, not noise.
  • Forward guidance stripped. The sentence “today’s action will support a more timely return of inflation to 2 percent” was replaced with “accumulating more information will help the Committee determine the appropriate stance of policy to ensure inflation returns to its 2 percent objective in a timely manner.” This is the specific linguistic signal that Warsh is positioning the committee to leave options open on the December decision, consistent with his pre‑meeting hints that he wants to see one more inflation print before committing.

Warsh in the press conference

  • “2% is a hard target.” The specific framing to carry: Warsh is anchoring the inflation target as non‑negotiable, which rules out a dovish surprise in the near term and keeps December as a live hike meeting.
  • “Monetary medicine is starting to work.” The specific defence of the September hike as the appropriate action, which is a specific message to Cook and Hammack that he thinks the policy stance is where it should be for one more meeting of data gathering.
  • Declined forward guidance for December. “I’m not going to prejudge the next meeting.” This is the specific message that the CME FedWatch 80%+ December hike probability is the market’s call, not Warsh’s.
  • Politics played “no role” in the hold. Specific pre‑empt against the Trump‑appointment criticism that Warsh is behind the president’s dovish line. The denial is the specific confirmation that he is anchored to inflation data, not to the midterms (3 November, six days away).
  • Fewer rate‑setting meetings from 2027. The structural announcement: eight FOMC meetings per year is confirmed as the right number, but one of the eight starting 2027 will focus exclusively on monetary strategy with no rate vote and no policy statement. This is a Warsh‑specific signalling reform — the Jackson Hole analogue inside the FOMC schedule — and it will take effect from his first full year of chairmanship.

Market pricing after

The market reaction was subdued. US indices closed in a tight range: S&P +0.28% to 7,743.98, Nasdaq +0.27% to 26,549.85, Dow +0.3% approximately to 52,500. The 10‑2 split with two hawkish dissents did not move the December pricing meaningfully — CME FedWatch December hike probability still ~80%+, consistent with Tuesday’s pricing. The 10Y Treasury yield drifted a touch higher post the hold on the statement upgrade; the dollar firmed modestly on the hawkish dissents, which is partly what pushed GBP/USD to the 1.3286 close.

Transmission to your book

The hawkish‑hold base case was already priced; the delivery met expectations with a slightly more hawkish tilt from the two dissents. For GSK and UKW, both long‑duration proxies, the Fed outcome is modestly supportive at the margin — the lack of a surprise hike means the duration repair of the last two weeks continues, but the hawkish dissents keep a lid on long‑end repricing. For META, GOOGL, MSFT and the chip book, the delivery is neutral — the AI capex story is now so dominant that marginal Fed moves are no longer the primary variable. For Shell, the muted dollar move keeps the dollar sleeve cushion essentially unchanged on the Shell day; the GBP/USD at 1.3286 vs Tuesday’s 1.3315 is −0.22%, approximately −£30 to −£60 on the ~£93k dollar book.

04Healey Budget: milder than feared, specific items that bind the book

Three specific items in the Red Book remove political overhangs on your book: CGT rate rises explicitly rejected, cash ISA U‑turn to £20k, bank surcharge temporary and smaller than feared. The energy‑tax architecture confirmed EPL to 2030 and set the OGRL trigger at $90/90p. EGL rose to 55% as previously signalled, with a WCfD opt‑in path for UKW.

The items that bind the direct book

  • CGT rate rises explicitly rejected. Healey publicly rejected a CGT rate rise — the specific political overhang on the Shell exit decision from the II General wrapper is removed. The CGT‑on‑gain gate still binds (the +176% on cost on 195 shares makes any exit a six‑figure CGT event even at current 24% rates against her annual exempt amount), but the risk of a near‑term rate step higher is now off the table.
  • Cash ISA U‑turn: stays at £20k. The previously announced cut to £12k from April 2027 is scrapped. For the T212 ISA and II ISA sleeves this is directly material: she can continue to fill £20k of overall ISA each year, with the sub‑limit split restored to the full flexibility. No action required on the sleeve configuration.
  • Bank surcharge: 3→6% for 15 months only (1 Jan 2027 to 31 Mar 2028), then back to 3%. Total expected raise £1.48bn across three fiscal years. The market feared 8% permanent; the actual outcome is much less bad — which is why Lloyds +1.7%, NatWest +2% and Barclays +1.6% all rallied on the news. She does not hold any UK banks directly, but the sector read sets the Thursday morning Lloyds Q3 and Friday NatWest Q3 into a less‑hostile political backdrop.
  • Mansion tax threshold cut rumour: rejected. The £2m threshold on the High Value Council Tax Surcharge stays. Not directly material to any holding, but the specific signal is that the Budget has stopped short of the most aggressive wealth‑tax rumours.
  • Property and savings income tax +2pp from April 2027. This is on top of the previously planned +2pp, taking top rates to 24% / 44% / 49%. For the ISA sleeve this is irrelevant — the ISA shields exactly this income. For the II General wrapper (BRK‑B and SHEL L), any dividend income held outside the ISA will now be taxed at the raised rate; Shell on 195 shares pays approximately £240 of gross dividends per quarter, which is below her annual dividend allowance when combined with GAW’s £10.50. BRK‑B pays no dividend. Minor practical bind.
  • Income tax threshold freeze through April 2031. OBR estimates £55bn+ fiscal drag by 2030/31. For her as an individual the frozen thresholds continue to pull more of her earned income into the higher‑rate band each year; the ISA sleeve is still the primary shelter.
  • Pensions pulled into IHT scope. OBR estimates £1.5bn/year by 2030. Material to estate planning in the long run; not direct to the current book.
  • State pension +3.9% to approximately £250.70/week; new Pension Tax Allowance so pensioners with income up to the full state pension pay no income tax. Not direct to her; relevant to the family.

The energy‑tax architecture

  • EPL stays at 38% through April 2030. The industry push to end EPL in 2026 was rejected. Shell UKCS continues on the 38% levy for the next 3.5 years. Status quo; not a worsening.
  • OGRL at 35% from April 2030 with price‑activated triggers: $90/bbl for oil and 90p/therm for gas, both inflation‑indexed annually. The specific framing: Shell UKCS integrated gas segment at current UK gas prices (218–224p/therm) is ~2.4x the trigger, so under the post‑2030 regime the levied share rises. The UKCS integrated gas production is approximately 5–7% of group profit, so the group‑level drag is marginal, but the Thursday Q3 commentary may touch on how Shell is modelling the regime shift.
  • EGL raised 45→55% from 1 July 2026 on exceptional generation receipts above the strike. UKW is subject to EGL on its RO‑scheme generation. The step up to 55% is partially offset by the parallel introduction of a voluntary Wholesale CfD scheme: existing generators not already contracted under a CfD can opt in for a fixed strike price, in exchange for being exempt from EGL. For UKW, which operates mostly under RO, the opt‑in decision becomes a specific capital‑allocation question: fixed‑price revenue security vs upside from the current £180/MWh Winter 26 forward curve. RBC Capital Markets has previously estimated the current forward‑power curve adds approximately £150m of additional cash flow for UKW over three years — opting into a WCfD at the strike would surrender that upside. Expect the UKW IM‑day commentary (or a specific RNS) to address this trade‑off.
  • CfDs including the Wholesale CfD, and all new investment, are exempt from EGL. For UKW’s AR8‑contracted new‑build pipeline, the regime is clean.
  • North Sea tax revenue +25% to £5.1bn per OBR — the specific fiscal reason EPL survives at 38% through 2030: the current oil and gas price strip is simply too valuable to leave untaxed.

  • FTSE 100 closed +7.96pts (+0.1%) at 10,694.50 — a flat day in which banks (Lloyds +1.7%, NatWest +2%, Barclays +1.6%, HSBC bounce) offset housebuilders (lower on BTL tax tightening) and pharma (GSK −4.58%, AstraZeneca lower on sector sympathy).
