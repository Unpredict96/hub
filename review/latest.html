01

Meta’s Muse app broke the CPU trade wide open — AMD +12.7% in a week

Positive≈£53,000 look-through

The story of the week. Muse, Meta’s agentic app that launched around 10 September, went to #1 on the US App Store roughly two weeks in and stayed there. Sell-side finally worked out what its architecture means for silicon: each Muse instance runs on its own secure cloud VM — a full browser, a CPU, memory, storage, agent keeps executing after the user closes the app. GPUs do model reasoning, but browsing, tool use, code execution and orchestration all sit on CPUs, and the units scale per active agent, not per user. That is a step-change repricing of the CPU footprint under inference.

Monday priced it violently. AMD +8.9% intraday to $609.38, an all-time high, briefly through a $1trn market cap. Intel +12–14%, best S&P performer. Arm +13–17%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index +3.9%, its fifth consecutive up session. The rally then held through a bond rout that would normally have killed it: AMD closed the week at $630.63, +12.7% in dollars, +12.2% in sterling against your book. Meta itself finished at $751.66, +12.9% in dollars. This is the trade under Nvidia — Nvidia added only 1.3% in dollars, and the H20 licensing question left unanswered by the Trump–Xi summit weighed on it separately (item 03).

The look-through is where this lands. AMD is 2.64% of ATT (adds about £12), a top-two weight in SMH (a +6.6% week to £722), and sits inside every global tracker you own. Meta is 3.88% of ATT, index weight everywhere else. On the £53,000 of semi-and-AI look-through the sleeve added on the order of £600–900 across the week before separately taking Thursday’s Nasdaq −1.13% on the yield move. The net was still comfortably positive: ATT itself printed +£254 (+5.69%) and touched a fresh 52-week high on Tuesday intraday.

02

The 30-year hit a 2004 high and the 10-year cleared 5% for good

Negative100% of book indirectly · £9,401 direct

The bond move was the second largest event of the week, and it landed harder than the tape wanted to admit. Wednesday’s US flash composite PMI printed 58.4 — the strongest private-sector expansion since July 2021, services 58.7, manufacturing 57.0, new orders 58.2 — and the input-prices sub-index hit 66.4, the highest since October 2022. Growth and inflation together, the specific combination Warsh has said would justify another hike. A poor five-year Treasury auction into it produced the biggest single-session 10Y sell-off in nearly 18 months. 10Y +14bp to 5.116%, first sustained print above 5% since July 2007; 5Y through 5% for the first time since 2007.

Thursday broadened the move. Initial jobless claims fell to 197k, adding a labour-market data point saying growth is not the problem, and the sell-off extended. 30Y +6bp to 5.462%, the highest since 2004. Markets now price well over 90% odds of a 25bp hike on 9 December. The Nasdaq took two down sessions in a row (−1.11% Wednesday, −1.13% Thursday) before Friday’s modest rebound. The Russell 2000 −1.60% on Wednesday was the clean tell: this was a duration event, not a growth scare.

UKW carried the direct hit. The trust does not trade on gilt yields tick for tick, but its 8–9% discount rate references them. UKW −1.83% on the week to 112.60p, −£175 on the position. The QT rewrite from a fortnight ago has not been undone, so the structural bull case for the discount is intact; the near-term catalyst is the 15 October Capital Markets Event, and until then this line is just going to trade with the long end.

03

Trump and Xi shook hands. The tape called it relief, not resolution.

Mixed≈£5,600 direct/thematic

The state dinner on Thursday landed roughly where Reuters had trailed it. The tariff truce that expired 10 November was extended by two months to 10 January 2027; China suspended rare-earth export controls in exchange for a US tariff reduction on some Chinese goods and a Chinese agricultural purchase commitment. The guest list — Cook, Huang, Altman, Musk, Bezos, Dell, Fraser, Pichai — delivered the imagery. What was not announced is the material part for your book: no Nvidia H20 export licensing update, no semiconductor equipment controls decision, no Taiwan-related statement. The Diplomat’s headline, “Relief Without a Resolution,” is fair.

DiDi — the position that ran 4% into the summit on nothing — gave it back. DIDIY −2.5% in dollars to $3.59, −2.86% in sterling, −£41 on the position. The pattern is now clean: this line trades summit expectations, not fundamentals, and when the summit delivers a tactical rather than structural outcome it fades. EMQP behaved similarly (−0.85% on the week). Rare-earth suspension is a two-month reprieve; China still processes ~90% of the world’s rare earths, and that leverage is unchanged.

The Nvidia read is the one to hold onto. Huang was in the room, the H20 file did not move, and NVDA closed the week only +0.83% in sterling against AMD’s +12%. The market is telling you the summit was a modest headwind for NVIDIA specifically because the export question is now definitively unresolved into the Q3 print. Kress selling $7.65m of stock on 17 September carries a small amount of weight against that backdrop.
