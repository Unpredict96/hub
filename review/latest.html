2 What actually moved the book

Ranked by how much of the portfolio each item touches. The Shell rule is the only one with an action attached, and it comes fifth.

01 · Global equities retraced most of last weekPositive~£136k touched

SPY went from $689.81 to $728.32 in a Thanksgiving-shortened week, +5.58%, against −8.4% the week before. AP called it the S&P’s best week in three years. The path: Monday +4.72% on the Huang reinstatement (Nasdaq-100 +6.86%, tech +9.0%), then three down or flat sessions on Goldman’s “not a clearing event”, the Section 301 tariff proposal and the Nvidia legal headlines, then +1.61% in Friday’s half day to 7,298. The Nasdaq briefly traded back in a bear market intraday on Wednesday. In Europe the Stoxx 600 rose about 3% to 643.85, after its worst week since March. The FTSE rose over 3% to 10,699, led by miners on copper’s best day in ten months, with pharma and energy lagging.

What this touches: every fund line. The London ETFs rose 3.1–3.2% (+£889). The OEICs rose about 3% on the market (about +£3.2k) before the re-mark. Rathbone, the growth tilt, rebounded hardest, as it fell hardest. Fidelity European lagged because Europe recovered less than the US, and the Spanish election is on Sunday. Fundsmith lagged the trackers, which is consistent with its lower AI-chain weight. One thing doesn’t fit the “relief” story: gold rose 1.7% on Friday to about $3,627 while yields rose, so the hedge bid hasn’t gone away.

02 · Nvidia: reinstatement up, everything after it downMixedHigh · ~£5.1k + chain

The board unanimously reinstated Huang on Sunday night, six days after saying it wouldn’t, following public pressure from the President. NVDA closed $165.05 (+31.48%) on Monday. The rest of the week was a list of new problems. Tue: the WSJ reported that Huang disclosed further affairs to the board before the vote, and Commerce added Megaspeed and six other South-East Asian entities to the Entity List ($155.49, −5.8%). Wed: S&P put the AA rating on CreditWatch Negative. Nvidia reportedly told partners to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia (WSJ, FT), the first item that touches revenue rather than reputation. Trump said he would pardon Huang “if needed” ($149.30, −3.9%). Fri: Bloomberg traced a GB300 NVL72 rack to a Shanghai data centre, the first public evidence that export-controlled Blackwell systems are physically in China ($150.49, +0.8%, against TSM +3.2%).

The week ended at +19.9%, all of it from Monday. The 10-Q is still unfiled. I found no NT 10-Q and no SEC update, and the secondary-source deadline is about 4 December. The signal worth keeping from Friday: the rest of the chain rose on a day of bad Nvidia news. If that holds through the testimony and the filing, the fund sleeve’s tail risk is smaller than last week’s review assumed.

What this touches: the £275 direct line, the T212 ISA chain (£4.6k), and about £4.85k of Nvidia through the funds, up from £4.05k only because the stock rose 20%. See s5.

03 · Rates: hotter data, and Treasuries held anywayMixedMedium · UKW + UK fund weight

US: October core PCE 3.3% y/y (3.2% expected), Q3 GDP revised to 4.3%, claims 189k. A December Fed hike is near-certain, and January is “under consideration”. The 10-year still fell to about 4.81% intraday on Wednesday as tariffs and the chain sell-off outweighed the data, the first session in this drawdown where bonds hedged equities. It gave that back on Friday, closing at 4.94%. UK: Ofgem set the January–March cap at £2,166 (+26%). On my arithmetic that adds about 0.9–1.0pp to January CPI. Bailey and Lombardelli leaned dovish on the peak. Mann and Greene leaned the other way. A 17 December hike is about 80% priced. The 10-year gilt held 5.16–5.19% all week. Sterling: 1.3086 to 1.3095, after touching 1.3010 on Monday, its lowest since November 2025. That is neutral for the £80k-plus of dollar exposure.

What this touches: UKW (+0.8%, back to within 1% of cost). The cap supports its power-price revenue and the NAV, but it also makes a hike more likely, which raises the discount rate. The shares tracked risk appetite more than gilts. It also touches the UK weight in LifeStrategy and Royal London.

04 · The Section 301 tariff proposalNegativeLow so far · broad

On Wednesday USTR proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 partners: China +20% rising to 40%, Taiwan and Vietnam 35%, plus the EU, Japan and Korea. Greer says none of it bites for months. It was part of Wednesday’s sell-off and the bond rally, then got lost in Friday’s bounce. For the book, the open question is whether semiconductors are carved out, as they have been before. That is a TSM question first, and it touches Fidelity European through exporters. I don’t know the answer yet.

China approved bepirovirsen for chronic hepatitis B, and depemokimab’s Phase III OCEAN trial in EGPA met its primary endpoint, with filing planned for H1 2027. GSK rose 5.4% on the week, the best UK line, and reversed most of last week’s unexplained drift.

08 · European gas: storage and price divergedNeutral for the bookYour job, not your book

EU withdrawals ran at 5.7, 6.5 and 7.1 TWh/d on 23–25 November. Storage is 66.6% (gas day 26), 3.2pt below the ten-year low for the date, and Germany is 53.75%. TTF still fell from Monday’s €96.08 intraday high to about €84.5 on Friday, on Norway’s return, an Atlantic arb shut to Asia in every month Spark assesses, and positioning. NBP held a 3–5p premium all week, which reads as UK demand. The book’s only link is Shell’s leg 1 (TTF > €60), which is wide. The dailies’ view stands: the sell-off is a December trade, and the storage arithmetic comes back into the price by Q1. There is no instrument in these accounts that expresses it.
