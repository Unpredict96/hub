2 What actually moved the book

Ranked by how much of the portfolio each item touches, not by the size of the headline. The Anthropic IPO got the most coverage this week and comes nearly last.

01 · The AI capex chain sold off as one tradeNegative~£115k touched

The S&P lost 8.4% in dollars over five straight down sessions (SPY: Mon −1.92%, Tue −1.02%, Wed −0.23%, Thu −1.96%, Fri −3.58%). On Friday it closed about 11.5% below its 6 October record, in a correction, and the Dow was in one too. The Stoxx 600 lost 5.8%, its worst week since March. The FTSE 100 lost about 5.1%. This was not a broad sale of growth. Lilly fell 0.3% on the day QQQ fell 5.5%. The market sold the chain: chips, lithography, foundry and AI power. GEV fell 7.7% on Friday with no company news, which is the clearest example. The week also had a Hunterbrook short report on AMD (Thu, −7.7%, then −13.3% Fri) and a BIS rule closing off rented compute for Chinese firms (Mon).

What this touches: every global fund line, about £115k excluding Fidelity European, with about £75–80k of US look-through. The fund sleeve is −£7,427 on the week (ETFs −£1,618 at verified prices, OEICs about −£5.8k estimated). Sterling’s 0.95% fall gave back roughly £700. Rathbone (growth tilt) is likely the weakest OEIC. Royal London and the two LifeStrategy lines, with their UK weight, are likely the strongest. Fidelity European has no US tech, but it fell with European banks and luxury, so it did not protect the book this week.

02 · Nvidia: beat, raise, then an accounting questionNegativeHigh · ~£4.3k direct + look-through

The sequence matters. Tue 17: Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8bn against a $108bn ±2% guide (+103% y/y). Data centre $107.6bn, adj. EPS $2.68 against about $2.48. Q4 guided to $130bn ±3%. That includes an extra week (about $120.7bn without it) and assumes zero China data-centre revenue. Gross margin is guided down to 71–72% from 74.9%. The 10-Q was not filed with results, after nine straight quarters of same-day filing, and the WSJ reported an SEC civil inquiry into whether disclosures misled investors. Wed 18: Kress raised the FY28 framing to about 75%, which cleared the last exit-rule condition. The stock reached $159.70, then closed at $150.96, below the pre-print close. Thu 19: $146.06, a post-Huang low. Fri 20: Reuters, citing two people briefed, reported SEC enforcement staff are looking at potential accounting issues. The stock fell 13.79% to $125.85, its worst day since DeepSeek, and 22.7% on the week.

What this touches: the £231 NVDA line (−37% on cost), the rest of the T212 ISA chain, and about £4.05k of Nvidia held through the funds. The next hard data point is the 10-Q, which a secondary source puts due around 4 December. If it is filed clean, much of Friday’s move can reverse. An NT 10-Q or an auditor qualification would be a separate and worse event.

03 · Brent tested the Shell trigger and heldNear missHigh · £6,970 + the trim rule

Brent settled at $95.84 on 13 Nov and $92.57 on 20 Nov, −3.4% and its third weekly fall. Daily settles: Mon $92.44, Wed $91.95, the closest yet (after a 3.38m bbl US crude build), Thu $91.98, Fri $92.57. On Friday it traded down to about $90.06 (Trading Economics CFD) in the European morning, six cents from the line, then short-covering ahead of Thanksgiving week lifted it. The bearish case: Hormuz flows are back near pre-war levels, the IEA put Q4 at a 1.7 mb/d surplus, and Goldman expects a $88 average for 2027. Three Gulf headlines in a row (mine claims and the Khasab projectile) failed to move the settle. The only bid that worked was Tuesday’s US strike on Qeshm Island.

What this touches: SHEL directly (−3.9% WoW, −£283), and the decision the rule would force. A settle below $90.00 trims 25%, about 49 shares, roughly £1,750 of proceeds and about £1,060 of realised gain in II General. Leg 3 now has data, and it is soft. The Trump–Xi–Putin Shenzhen statement pledged more refined-product supply, and the implied diesel crack fell from about $70 to $65/bbl in two sessions. Leg 1 is wide, with TTF around €85.5.

04 · UK rates: CPI a tenth light, the BoE still hawkishMixedMedium · UKW, GSK, UK fund weight

Pill said on Monday the BoE has “further to go”, and the 2-year gilt touched 5.015%, its highest since 2023. October CPI on Wednesday was 3.6% against 3.7% expected (core 2.8%, services 3.7% against the BoE’s 3.9%). December hike pricing fell from about 90% to 80% and gilts rallied 78 ticks. October retail sales on Friday beat at +0.7% m/m against −0.1%, but that was households stockpiling under the “Be Ready” campaign, which is a food-inflation impulse rather than evidence of demand. The US 10-year fell back below 5% on Friday (about 4.95–4.97%) as money moved into safety.

What this touches: UKW (−1.4% WoW). It rose on CPI day, then fell 2.9% on Thursday and Friday even though yields fell, so the move was not about duration. Also GSK (−3.6%, still no news behind it) and the UK part of LifeStrategy and Royal London. Correction carried from Monday’s brief: the Budget was on 28 October. It is not still to come, as last week’s review said. The remaining UK duration event is the BoE on 17 December.
